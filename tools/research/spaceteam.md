# spaceteam — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

What I actually saw in the 5 App Store screenshots: the screen is three stacked zones. Top ~22% is a live space viewport — near-black navy (#000014) filled with horizontal motion-blur streaks (capsule shapes in #030350, #2A2A6E, #575779, lavender-white), a small 3/4-view grey-blue starship at right (hull #333843 with chrome highlights, three engine pods, a glowing cyan iris), and a raging supernova wall pinned to the LEFT edge (white core → #FFED78 → #FFF3B9 → orange → #9A312B) that is visibly chasing the ship. Middle is a charcoal instruction band with a soft vertical gradient (#141414 top → #282828 centre → #1A1A1A bottom), a solid white right-pointing triangle at the left margin, then the order in enormous white bold pixel-edged type wrapping to two lines ("Turn on Dangling Shunter", "Engage Eigenthrottle"). Below it a razor-thin (~1.3% height) full-width RIBBED progress bar: the filled part is finely vertically corrugated amber/olive (#C48901 or #7B9301, sine luminance ripple ±5%, period ~13px) and the empty part is the same corrugation at near-black (#050505). The bottom ~60% is the control panel: an irregular jigsaw of 4–6 unequal RECTANGULAR sub-panels tiled edge to edge, each in brushed light steel (#ADADAD–#B7B7B7) covered in a fine hexagonal dimple texture, separated by 1px #878787 seams, each with a Phillips screw head at all four corners. Each sub-panel carries a heavy black pixel-edged label sized to its own width — so a big panel gets huge type and a small one gets tiny type, and that dramatic mismatched scaling is the game's signature look. The damaged state (screenshot 4) washes the ENTIRE screen pink-red: steel becomes #E08080, black labels become dark maroon, and the star fills half the viewport.

- `#878787` — 1px panel seam and bezel shadow between sub-panels

- `#1A1A1A` — instruction band ground and all panel label ink
- `#FFFFFF` — order text, the ▶ cue triangle, spark cores, star core
- `#B20000` — red square push-button face (conic spun-metal sheen)
- `#730000` — button inner bevel shadow and OFF-state red
- `#C48901` — order timer / progress bar fill, amber mid-state
- `#7B9301` — progress bar fill, healthy olive-green state
- `#000014` — space viewport deep navy

## Typography

The original uses a chunky rounded BITMAP font at giant sizes — wide apertures, heavy weight, visible stair-stepping on every curve and diagonal, with real lowercase. Map it as a two-font system. (1) Space Grotesk 700 for the order text and all sub-panel labels: it is the only approved face that is geometric, very wide, heavily weighted and slightly odd in its terminals, which is what makes "Dangling Shunter" read as machine-stencilled rather than corporate. Use letterSpacing 0.5–1px and it sits almost on top of the reference. (2) Press Start 2P, sparingly and ONLY small, for the pixel chrome where the bitmap character actually lives: the "ON"/"OFF" caps inside red buttons, the 0/1/2/3 numerals above slider stops, dial detent numbers, and the LVL/HULL HUD strip. Never set the long order sentence in Press Start 2P — it has no lowercase and is unreadable under a 4-second timer. Explicitly reject: Bebas Neue (condensed, wrong — the reference is extremely wide), Inter/Nunito Sans (too neutral and friendly), the two serifs (wrong century), Space Mono (right era, wrong weight — usable only for a tiny stencilled panel serial like "PNL-4471").

## Shape language

Aggressively mismatched industrial hardware bolted into a rectilinear grid — that mismatch IS the design. Container language is strictly orthogonal: hard-edged rectangles, 1px seams, 2px insets, no rounded panel corners. Widget language is the opposite: circles, tapers, fans, knurls. Toggle switch = a black elliptical socket with a #9E9E9E rim and a white arc highlight on the upper-left, a chrome bat handle tapering outward filled with a HARD-BANDED perpendicular gradient (0 #FFF, 0.18 #D2D2D2, 0.45 #828282, 0.55 #6A6A6A, 0.8 #C8C8C8, 1 #4A4A4A), ending in a fluted cap drawn as a 10-spoke fan of alternating #E6E6E6/#8E8E8E wedges. Switches appear in several orientations (down = off, right = on, plus a vertical variant) and are never the same size twice. Red button = a rounded-rect (r=10) with a four-quadrant conic "spun metal" sheen brightest along the diagonals, a #730000 inner bevel bottom-right and #E24040 top-left, wrapped in a #B9A8A8→#6E5A5A bezel frame. Slider = a fat 46×64 chrome handle of 9 vertical bands on a thick pure-black rail with black tick marks. Screws are the connective tissue: r=6 circles, radial #D8D8D8→#9A9A9A, 1px #6E6E6E stroke, cross slot at a RANDOM angle per screw — that randomness is what makes it feel hand-assembled rather than vector-drawn. Later-level symbol labels are flat black clip-art silhouettes (fat d-pad cross, wireframe dodecahedron, 3.5" floppy, picnic table, catapult) with zero shading.

## UI chrome

No conventional UI chrome at all — the game IS the chrome. There are no rounded cards, no drop-shadowed dialogs, no icons-in-circles. The order band is a flat charcoal strip with a barely-there vertical gradient and a single white solid triangle as its only ornament. Progress/timer bars are full-bleed corrugated metal strips with zero border radius and no label. The HUD is essentially absent in the reference — no score, no timer digits, no player names on screen — all state is communicated by the bar's fill and hue, the star growing on the left, and the panel physically degrading (slime, sparks, blue plasma jets, panels tearing loose to reveal a dark-green circuit board with lighter traces and gold pads underneath). Buttons that are genuinely UI (menu, start) must be built from the same vocabulary: a steel sub-panel with corner screws and a red conic button set into it, never a rounded rectangle. Keep the total non-diegetic overlay to one 10px riveted divider strip carrying "LVL 4 · HULL 72%" in 9px Press Start 2P.

## Motion

Chunky, mechanical, snappy — nothing glides. Widget state changes are a 110–140ms ease-out with a single-frame overshoot then a hard settle, like a solenoid. Text NEVER cross-fades: a new order pops in at scale 1.15 → 1.0 over 60ms or it hard-cuts. A constant low-amplitude engine rumble translates the whole panel by (sin(t*23)*0.6, cos(t*17)*0.6) px so the screen is never perfectly still. Success on an order: that crew's half flashes rgba(200,255,200,0.25) for 90ms, a white ring expands out of the widget over 220ms, plus a metallic clunk. Expiry: that half shakes ±6px on x for 260ms at ~30Hz with exponential decay, a red vignette pulses in, klaxon. Hyperspace (1.4s): starfield capsules lerp to 6× width and 8× speed, current sub-panels fly outward with rotation and drop off-screen, then new ones fall in from off-screen and bounce-land 60ms apart, each with a clunk — the staggered landing is what sells "a new console has been bolted in". Damage overlays animate independently: sparks are 400–900ms tapered particles under gravity, the blue flame's radii wobble on sin(t*13), slime blobs grow and stretch drips over ~8s, loose panels drift and rotate at ~4°/s. The alarm wash pulses at 2Hz with amplitude driven by (1 − hull).

## Mechanics

ORIGINAL (confirmed): 2–8 players, one device each, local network. Every player gets a procedurally generated control panel of toggle switches, red push-buttons, sliders and rotary dials, each labelled with generated technobabble ("Dangling Shunter", "Photospoke", "Symmetric Gasket", "Gridthrust", "Subputty", "Eigenthrottle", "Macrodryer", "Spectrobolt", "Astral Synth", "Autospoon"). Each device shows ONE timed order at a time ("Turn on Dangling Shunter", "Engage Eigenthrottle", "Set Spectrobolt to 3", "Unbind Vaportool"). The order usually names a control on SOMEONE ELSE'S panel, so you shout it while simultaneously listening for orders aimed at you. No turns; everything is simultaneous. Enough completed orders fills a progress bar and the ship jumps to hyperspace: brand-new randomly generated panels for everyone, tighter timers, new anomalies — mislabeled instruments, labels replaced by abstract symbols, green slime spreading, sparks and blue plasma fires, instruments coming loose and swinging, wormholes (everyone flips their device upside down) and asteroids (everyone shakes). Missed/wrong orders damage the ship; too much damage and the pursuing star goes supernova and the run ends. Endless escalating levels, no win condition.

EXACT RULES FOR THE REBUILD:
• WIDGETS AND LEGAL ORDERS. TOGGLE (2 states, up/down or left/right) → "Flip {N} up/down", "Turn on/off {N}", "Engage/Disengage {N}". BUTTON (2 states, caption ON/OFF, tap toggles) → "Turn on/off {N}", "Press/Slam {N}". SLIDER (4 stops 0–3, drag-snap) → "Set/Slide/Crank {N} to {k}". DIAL (4–6 detents, drag-rotate) → "Turn/Dial/Rotate {N} to {k}". Flavour verbs from the original mapping onto toggle/button: Unbind, Prime, Buckle, Grease, Kickstart, Reticulate.
• NAME GENERATOR. PREFIX (Dangling, Photo, Symmetric, Grid, Sub, Eigen, Macro, Spectro, Quasi, Poly, Astral, Grip, Star, Auto, Vapor, Techno, Cryo, Neo, Ultra, Infra, Hyper, Micro, Retro, Omni, Turbo, Chrono, Baryon, Plasma, Fusion, Nano, Xeno, Volt, Iso, Mega, Proto, Trans, Flux, Servo, Halo) × STEM (Shunter, Spoke, Gasket, Thrust, Putty, Throttle, Dryer, Bolt, Puffer, Turbine, Synth, Vent, Mesh, Spoon, Tool, Vortex, Coupler, Sprocket, Valve, Winder, Clamp, Piston, Wafer, Bearing, Grommet, Ratchet, Spindle, Flange, Rotor, Baffle, Nozzle, Gimbal, Truss, Latch, Dampener, Injector, Lattice, Prong, Crank). 60% single word ("Eigenthrottle"), 40% adjective+stem ("Dangling Shunter") from ADJ (Dangling, Symmetric, Artificial, Bilateral, Molten, Wobbly, Reverse, Auxiliary, Damp, Perforated, Inverted, Redundant, Sticky, Blunt). HARD CONSTRAINTS: all names unique across BOTH panels; deliberately plant 1–2 confusable pairs per level sharing a prefix or stem across the two crews (Photospoke vs Photoputty) to force careful listening; cap at 16 chars for a small sub-panel.
• ORDER ISSUE. Each crew always holds exactly one active order (two queued from L6). The target control comes from the OPPOSING crew's panel with p=0.75, own panel p=0.25. The requested value must differ from the control's current value — never issue a no-op. The two crews' live orders must never target the same control.
• FULFILMENT. An order completes the instant the named control's value EQUALS the requested value, regardless of who touched it or how many intermediate values were passed through. On completion: +1 progress, success flash, next order after 250ms.
• ORDER TIMER. T = clamp(9.0 − 0.55×(level−1), 3.2, 9.0) seconds. The ribbed bar drains toward the crew's own edge; fill hue lerps #7B9301 → #BFA902 → #C48901 → #B20000 as it empties. On expiry: hull −14, the failed order stays on screen greyed with a red strike for 400ms (so players learn what they missed), then a new one issues. Hard expire — no grace window.
• WRONG TOUCHES. Changing the value of a control that is not the target of either live order costs 0.6s off BOTH crews' timers and buzzes that half red; it does NOT damage the hull (the original is forgiving here — the fun is panic, not punishment).
• HULL. Starts 100. Order expiry −14; failed all-hands anomaly −20; +5 restored per hyperspace jump (cap 100). Hull ≤ 0 → supernova animation, run ends.
• PROGRESS. Level L needs ordersNeeded = 8 + 3×L completed orders across both crews. On reaching it: HYPERSPACE — all live orders cancelled with no penalty, both panels destroyed and fully regenerated, level++, one new anomaly type unlocked.
• REGENERATION GUARANTEES. 4–6 sub-panels per crew (count = clamp(4 + floor(level/2), 4, 6)); at least 1 slider or dial per crew (so "set to N" orders exist) and at least 2 toggles.
• ANOMALY SCHEDULE. None in L1, never in the first 12s of a level, never during hyperspace. From L2 one fires every 18–28s and resolves in 6–12s. Unlock order: L2 SLIME, L3 BLACKOUT, L4 LOOSE PANEL, L5 WORMHOLE, L6 ASTEROIDS, L7 permanent SYMBOL LABELS, L8+ two concurrent. SLIME: a growing green blob covers 1–2 widgets on one crew's panel; that crew scrubs it off with a finger (an 8×8 wetness grid cleared by pointer moves) while orders keep coming. BLACKOUT: one crew's labels go dark for 8s — they must ask the OTHER crew what their controls are called, inverting the shouting direction. LOOSE PANEL: one sub-panel detaches and drifts/rotates over its neighbours; its widgets stay live. WORMHOLE: for 10s both halves render an EXTRA 180° rotation so each crew reads its own order upside down; both crews tap their WORMHOLE pad simultaneously to exit early. ASTEROIDS: an all-hands bar appears; both halves must hold their pad simultaneously for 2.5s (this replaces device shake, which is permission-gated and unreliable on mobile web). Failing to clear an all-hands anomaly before its window closes costs 20 hull.
• SCORE for the platform leaderboard: level×1000 + ordersCompleted×50 + hullRemaining×10.

## One-phone adaptation

TWO CREWS, ONE PHONE, FLAT ON THE TABLE, SPLIT-BRIDGE. The 2–8 humans divide into CREW ALPHA and CREW BETA (auto-balanced at setup) and sit on the two SHORT ends of the portrait phone, facing each other across it. The screen splits at y=50% with a 10px riveted divider. The bottom half is drawn upright for Alpha; the top half is drawn rotated 180° about its own centre for Beta. Both halves run SIMULTANEOUSLY — no turns, exactly like the original.

Layout inside each half (in its own rotated local space, W × 0.5H): 0–6% a mini space viewport strip (star chasing from the left, ship, streaks — duplicated in each half so both crews see the threat); 6–15.5% the charcoal order band with the ▶ triangle and the order in ~30px Space Grotesk 700 auto-fit to two lines; 15.5–17.5% the ribbed order timer draining toward that crew's own edge; 17.5–50% that crew's 4–6 sub-panel BSP control grid. The divider strip carries "LVL 4 · HULL 72%" rendered TWICE back to back in 9px Press Start 2P so both sides can read it.

Why this preserves the shouting: 75% of Alpha's orders name a control that lives on BETA's panel, and vice versa, at the same time. Alpha physically cannot reach across the phone fast enough under a 4-second timer, so they must shout it across the table. That is Spaceteam's exact core mismatch, reproduced without a second device.

HIDDEN INFORMATION WITHOUT PASS-THE-PHONE: your order is set near YOUR edge, so it sits ~13cm away, 180° rotated and small from the opposing crew's seat — and they are simultaneously being screamed at by their own timer. Reinforce with the deliberately confusable name pairs so a stolen glance is slower than just listening. This is soft privacy by geometry, and it is the right trade: the original is a shouting party game, not a hidden-role game, so partial leakage costs nothing. NO pass-the-phone privacy screen is needed anywhere in the core loop — the only variant that would need one is a saboteur mode, deliberately omitted.

INPUT ZONES NEVER OVERLAP: Alpha's fingers stay in y > 0.5H, Beta's in y < 0.5H, physically separated by the divider. Use pointerdown/move/up with per-pointerId tracking and touch-action:none so both crews act at the same instant; for the top half transform the point to (W−x, 0.5H−y) before hit-testing. On a 390×844 phone each half is 390×422, so 4–6 widgets give ~120×110px targets — never exceed 6 widgets per half.

SCALING 2 → 8: at 2 it is one person per side (still shout, or reach across and lose the race to the timer). At 3–4, one or two per side crowded at each end. At 5–8, each crew nominates one HANDS (the only person who should touch that half) while the rest are EYES who read the order aloud and shout it across; the crew's corner shows "HANDS: {name}" and it rotates automatically at every hyperspace jump so everyone gets a turn on the console. The game displays the role but does not enforce it — enforcement is social, which is the point.

Setup and end screens render twice, back to back in both orientations: crew-size picker (2–8), auto-balanced Alpha/Beta assignment, optional names via an on-canvas keyboard (no DOM inputs), then a 3-2-1 countdown visible to both ends. The game-over supernova and final score also render in both orientations.

## Drawing it with no assets

Zero assets — every pixel from Canvas 2D primitives, gradients, Path2D and cached pattern tiles.
• HEX DIMPLE STEEL: build one 24×28px tile in an OffscreenCanvas (legal — document.createElement is banned, OffscreenCanvas is not; fall back to a cached Path2D dimple grid if unsupported). Fill #B2B2B2, then a hex-offset grid of r=5 circles each filled with a tiny radial gradient rgba(255,255,255,0.10) → rgba(0,0,0,0.06). Use ctx.createPattern(tile,'repeat') as every sub-panel fill, then overlay a top→bottom linear gradient rgba(255,255,255,0.10) → transparent → rgba(0,0,0,0.10) for the sheen.
• PANEL SUBDIVISION: guillotine/BSP. Start with the panel rect, repeatedly split the largest region at 35–65% along its longer axis until N regions, enforcing a 90×90 CSS-px minimum so widgets always fit. Each region: 2px inset, 1px #878787 stroke, 4 corner screws.
• SCREW: arc r=6, radial gradient #D8D8D8 (offset 0.35) → #9A9A9A, 1px #6E6E6E stroke, then two 2px #5A5A5A rounded rects crossed at a RANDOM per-screw angle.
• TOGGLE: ellipse socket with radial #101010→#2E2E2E, 3px #9E9E9E rim, white arc highlight upper-left; shaft as a tapering quad filled by a gradient PERPENDICULAR to the shaft with hard chrome banding (0 #FFF, .18 #D2D2D2, .45 #828282, .55 #6A6A6A, .8 #C8C8C8, 1 #4A4A4A); end cap = circle with a 10-wedge fan of alternating #E6E6E6/#8E8E8E. Animate by lerping the shaft angle over 120ms with overshoot.
• RED BUTTON: roundRect r=10 filled with ctx.createConicGradient centred in the button, stops cycling #B20000 → #6E0000 four times to produce the X-shaped spun-metal sheen; FALLBACK for older Safari: 48 thin triangular wedges with alpha = |cos(2θ)|. Inner bevel #730000 bottom-right / #E24040 top-left; outer bezel a #B9A8A8→#6E5A5A frame. Caption "ON"/"OFF" in Press Start 2P black, 2px letterSpacing. Pressed = 18% darker, bevel inverted, content +2px.
• SLIDER: 12px pure-black roundRect rail, black tick marks, Press Start 2P numerals above; handle = 46×64 roundRect painted with 9 alternating vertical bands #FFFFFF/#C0C0C0/#7A7A7A, 1px #555 outline, drop shadow. Snap to nearest stop over 90ms with a detent tick.
• DIAL: radial #E2E2E2→#9C9C9C body, 18 short radial knurl lines around the rim, black pointer wedge, external ticks + numerals. Angle from touch centre, snapped to detents.
• SPACE VIEWPORT: 4 parallax layers of horizontal capsules (roundRects, w 20–160px) at random y in #030350/#2A2A6E/#575779/#9AA0FF scrolling right→left at layer speed; hyperspace lerps width ×6 and speed ×8 for 1.2s. STAR: 4 stacked offset radial gradients (#FFFFFF → #FFF3B9 → #FFED78 → #E07A20 → #9A312B → transparent) with per-frame jitter plus ~20 animated flare arcs; radius grows as hull falls. SHIP: one Path2D of bezier segments — rounded wedge hull with a vertical #6E7686 → #333843 → #1A1D24 gradient, three engine cylinders trailing #7FE9FF, chrome canopy ring, radial #FFFFFF → #35C8FF → #0A4A7A iris.
• RIBBED BARS: one 13px-wide pattern tile whose luminance is a sine; tint by drawing the tile then compositing the state colour with 'multiply'. Empty section = same tile at 6% brightness.
• SPARKS: 40-particle pool, each a 3-point tapered path with a gradient #FFFFFF → rgba(240,205,159,0), spawned upward from a seam under gravity, 400–900ms life.
• BLUE FLAME: three stacked vertically-scaled radial gradients (white core → #4371FF → rgba(168,215,255,0)) with radii wobbling on sin(t*13), plus one large low-alpha radial drawn in 'lighter' composite for the halo — no canvas filter needed anywhere.
• SLIME: metaball union of 7 lerping circles traced as a single path, flat #116E11 with a #1E8C1E highlight arc and 3 quadratic-curve drips that elongate over time; clipped to the uncleaned cells of an 8×8 wetness grid.
• SYMBOL LABELS: ~14 inline SVG path strings (d-pad cross, wireframe dodecahedron, 3.5" floppy, picnic table, catapult, umbrella, trumpet, anchor, teacup, cactus, pyramid, whisk, boomerang, tooth) rendered via new Path2D(d) and filled/stroked flat black — matching the reference's clip-art look with zero bytes of image data.
• ALARM WASH: a full-screen rect in 'multiply' with rgba(255,120,120,a) plus a 'screen' pass of rgba(255,0,0,0.12), a = f(hull) pulsing at 2Hz — reproduces the damaged red screenshot without any blur.
• AUDIO (optional, still zero assets): WebAudio only — clunk = 12ms noise burst through a lowpass; success = 900Hz square blip; klaxon = two detuned sawtooths; detent = a 40Hz-modulated click.

## Risks

- Eight humans genuinely cannot all reach one phone — mitigated by the two-crew split plus the rotating HANDS/EYES role, but 6+ is always a huddle; playtest whether the EYES stay engaged.
- Hidden info is protected only by rotation and distance, so a determined player CAN read the opposing crew's order and skip the shouting; confusable names and timer pressure make it slower than listening, but this is soft privacy, not real privacy.
- Long technobabble in a small BSP sub-panel will overflow — a measureText fit-and-wrap that drops to two lines and shrinks to an 11px floor is mandatory, and the generator must cap names at 16 chars for small regions.
- No getBoundingClientRect allowed: canvas coords must come from canvas.width/height, window.innerWidth/innerHeight and devicePixelRatio with the canvas pinned at 0,0 — any drift breaks every hit test on the rotated top half.
- Simultaneous two-crew input needs per-pointerId pointer events with touch-action:none; anything built on click or single-touch assumptions silently locks out one crew while the other is touching.
- createConicGradient is missing on older Safari (killing the signature spun-metal button) and OffscreenCanvas may be absent for texture tiles — ship the 48-wedge |cos(2θ)| and cached-Path2D fallbacks, and cache static sub-panels offscreen so particles/slime/flame don't drop frames on low-end phones.
