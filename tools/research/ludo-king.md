# ludo-king — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

Bright, high-saturation, flat-vector "casual mobile" look sitting inside heavy skeuomorphic chrome — I confirmed this from Ludo King's own App Store screenshots. The board itself is almost entirely FLAT: pure white cells (#FFFFFF) with thin near-black hairline grid lines, four 6x6 corner yards in unshaded solid primaries (red #EB1C24 top-left, green #039F4B top-right, blue #24A5F6 bottom-left, yellow #FEE117 bottom-right), and zero gradients or texture on the playfield — all the visual richness is pushed onto the tokens and the surrounding UI. The board floats on a royal-blue radial gradient (#4A88EA centre falling to #0E3E7E at the corners) overlaid with a large, low-contrast tiled diagonal motif of dark-navy domino/dice tiles (#201F3F at ~25% contrast) that reads as wallpaper, never competing with the board. Chrome is the opposite register: fat gold (#FCD500) rounded-rect borders, deep navy (#003C94) panel fills, glossy bevels, drop shadows, and outlined display type — a 2016-era Indian casual-game aesthetic that is unapologetically loud. Tokens are the one 3D element: silver-white map-pin/teardrop markers with a coloured lens disc in the head, a specular highlight, and a dark maroon elliptical base ring. In the 6-player mode the whole board becomes a hexagon of six triangular yards radiating from a near-black (#08131A) centre hexagon that holds the die, and each player's nameplate is a rotated tab OUTSIDE the board oriented to face that seat — Ludo King already solved shared-screen orientation exactly this way.

## Typography

Ludo King's headers ("King of Ludo", "6 Players Ludo", "PLAY ONLINE", "PASS N PLAY") are a very heavy, slightly condensed all-caps sans with a thick black outline and a gold fill — of the eight approved fonts, **Bebas Neue** is the only real match and should carry every display surface: the title, player names on seat plates, big pip numbers, turn banners, and the win screen. Bebas Neue's condensed all-caps is also the single best choice for the rotated seat plates, because condensed glyphs fit in the narrow left/right edge strips of a portrait phone where a normal-width face would not. Pair it with **Nunito Sans** at 700/800 for everything else — HUD chips, token counts, roll history, rules text, seat-setup labels. Nunito Sans's rounded terminals and high x-height carry the same friendly casual-game warmth as the original's rounded UI type, and it stays legible at the 11-13px sizes the seat plates force. Explicitly reject: Press Start 2P (pixel-retro, wrong era), Cormorant Garamond and DM Serif Display (serifs — nothing in Ludo King is serif), Space Mono (technical), Inter (correct but characterless here — Nunito Sans is warmer and closer), Space Grotesk (acceptable third choice for numerals only if a slightly modern edge is wanted). Always render Bebas Neue display text with a manual outline: `ctx.lineWidth = fontSize*0.16; ctx.strokeStyle='#101820'; ctx.strokeText()` then `ctx.fillText()` — the black-outline treatment is load-bearing to the identity and it is also what makes the type survive being read upside-down across a table.

## Shape language

Ruthlessly orthogonal on the playfield, soft and rounded everywhere else. The board is a strict 15x15 square lattice — every cell is an unrounded square with a hairline stroke; the four yards are hard 6x6 squares; the home columns are straight 5-cell runs of colour; the centre is a 3x3 square split into four right triangles that meet at an exact point. The only curves inside the board are: the yard's inner white square (corner radius ~0.25 cell), the four circular token slots inside each yard, the stroked 5-pointed safe-square stars, and the tokens themselves. Tokens are the signature shape and they are NOT classic conical pawns — they are Google-Maps-style pins: a circle head with two tangent flanks converging to a point at the bottom, sitting on a squashed elliptical base ring. The die is a rounded square (radius ~0.22 of side) with circular pips. Surrounding chrome uses pill and rounded-rect language exclusively: seat plates are rounded rects with radius ~0.35 of height, the dice puck is a full circle, buttons are 12-16px-radius rects inside 5-6px gold frames. Direction arrows are thin, open, round-capped strokes (shaft plus two 40-degree head strokes), never filled triangles. The 6-player board swaps the square lattice for a radial one: a regular hexagon whose six 60-degree wedges each carry a trapezoidal band of quad cells generated by lerping between the wedge's two edges, with inward-pointing triangular yards on the outer rim.

## UI chrome

Every panel is the same recipe: a rounded rect filled with a vertical navy gradient (#0152BE top to #003C94 bottom), wrapped in a 5-6px solid gold border (#FCD500) with a 2px darker gold outer stroke (#B8930A) and a 1px inner highlight (rgba(255,255,255,0.35)) along the top edge only — that inner highlight is what sells the bevel. Drop shadow beneath: rgba(0,0,0,0.4), offsetY = 4px. The bottom HUD bar in-game is one such panel spanning the width, carrying the two player names in yellow (#F9F02F) Bebas Neue with black outline, a pin avatar at each end, and a raised dice tile in the dead centre. On the board, player nameplates are white Bebas Neue with a 3px black outline sitting directly on the coloured yard (4-player) or on rotated gold/coloured tabs projecting outside the board rim (6-player). Safe squares are stroked-only 5-point stars in mid-grey — never filled. Track entry points carry a small colour-matched arrow glyph in the outer cell of each home-column row. Coins/gems/level counters in the original live in dark navy pill chips with a gold "+" button on the right — reuse that chip for the roll-history strip. Turn indication in the original is a large gold-to-red gradient downward chevron pointing at the active player; keep it, but rotate it to point outward toward whichever seat is live. No thin lines, no subtle greys, no minimalism anywhere in the chrome — if an element does not have a border and a shadow it will look broken next to the rest.

## Motion

Chunky, physical, and generous — every action gets a beat, because on a shared screen the animation IS the communication channel. Token movement is a HOP, never a slide: the token traverses one cell at a time, ~110ms per cell, with a parabolic lift (yOffset = -0.55*cell*sin(pi*t)) and easeInOutQuad along the path; on each landing it squashes to scaleY 0.86 / scaleX 1.10 and recovers over 90ms. This is critical, not decorative — it lets six people around a table visually count the pips and trust the move. Dice roll: ~700ms total, face swapping on a decelerating schedule (16 swaps, interval ramping 30ms to 140ms), with a scale bounce 1.0 to 1.18 to 1.0 on easeOutBack and a damped rotation wobble of +/-0.12 rad; it lands with a hard stop and a single 120ms white flash. A six adds a gold ring pulse expanding from the die (r: 0 to 2.2x, alpha 1 to 0, 380ms) and three staggered rings from the active seat plate. Capture is the biggest moment: the attacker lands, a white impact ring snaps out from the cell (stroked, 5px to 1px, 260ms), the victim token pops to scale 1.25 (120ms, easeOutBack) then arcs home along a quadratic bezier over 420ms easeInOutCubic and drops into its slot with a squash. Reaching Home: token scales to 0 over 200ms while 14 procedural colour triangles burst radially with gravity, and the centre triangle of that colour flashes white for 150ms. Turn handoff: a coloured glow ring around the board cross-fades to the new player's colour over 300ms while the dice puck slides along the board rim to the new seat on easeOutCubic (450ms) and the arriving seat plate scales 1.0 to 1.06 to 1.0. Idle: legal tokens breathe at scale 1.0 to 1.06 on a 900ms sine, and the active seat plate has a slow 2s pulsing gold outer glow. Nothing snaps instantly; nothing takes longer than half a second except a full 6-cell walk.

## Mechanics

GEOMETRY (measured cell-by-cell off the real Ludo King board screenshot; rows/cols 0-14, row 0 top). Board is 15x15. Yards: RED rows0-5/cols0-5, GREEN rows0-5/cols9-14, BLUE rows9-14/cols0-5, YELLOW rows9-14/cols9-14. Track ring = all cells of the four 3x6 arms except the home columns = 13 cells per arm x 4 = exactly 52. Canonical ring order starting at RED's start square (index 0): (6,1)(6,2)(6,3)(6,4)(6,5)(5,6)(4,6)(3,6)(2,6)(1,6)(0,6)(0,7)(0,8)(1,8)(2,8)(3,8)(4,8)(5,8)(6,9)(6,10)(6,11)(6,12)(6,13)(6,14)(7,14)(8,14)(8,13)(8,12)(8,11)(8,10)(8,9)(9,8)(10,8)(11,8)(12,8)(13,8)(14,8)(14,7)(14,6)(13,6)(12,6)(11,6)(10,6)(9,6)(8,5)(8,4)(8,3)(8,2)(8,1)(8,0)(7,0)(6,0) then wraps to index 0. START squares: RED idx0 (6,1), GREEN idx13 (1,8), YELLOW idx26 (8,13), BLUE idx39 (13,6) — exactly 13 apart. HOME COLUMNS (5 cells, owner only): RED (7,1)-(7,5), GREEN (1,7)-(5,7), YELLOW (7,9)-(7,13), BLUE (9,7)-(13,7); the centre 3x3 holds four triangles meeting at the exact centre (top=green, left=red, bottom=blue, right=yellow) and is HOME. SAFE SQUARES: exactly 8 — the 4 start squares plus 4 stars at (2,6),(6,12),(12,8),(8,2); in ring indices safe = {0,8,13,21,26,34,39,47}. PATH MATH: for a player with start offset S, a token's relative position p runs 0..50 on the ring (absolute = (S+p) mod 52), p=51..55 are its 5 home-column cells, p=56 is HOME. Total 56 pips from start square to Home. The branch point is p=50, which is that player's own arrow cell.
RULES. 4 tokens each, all begin in the yard. One fair d6. Turn order is fixed clockwise by seat; highest opening roll goes first (re-roll ties). ENTERING: a token leaves the yard ONLY on a roll of 6 and is placed directly on its own start square (p=0). SIXES: a 6 always grants an extra roll, including when the 6 was unusable; three consecutive 6s in one turn voids that third roll entirely — it is discarded, the turn ends immediately, and play passes (moves already made on the first two sixes stand). EXTRA TURNS are granted for: rolling a 6, capturing an opponent token, and landing a token in HOME. Extra turns stack (capture with a 6 = one extra roll, not two — grant at most one bonus roll per resolved move, but the three-six counter only counts sixes). MOVEMENT: exactly the number rolled, forward. CAPTURE: landing on a cell holding exactly one opponent token returns it to its yard (must re-roll a 6 to re-enter). Capture is IMPOSSIBLE on any of the 8 safe squares — a token there is immune and the arriving token simply shares the cell. No captures inside home columns (opponents can never enter them). You never capture your own colour. BLOCKADE: two or more of your own tokens may share any cell; 2+ same-colour tokens form a block that opponents may neither land on NOR pass through (make this a settings toggle — the softer variant blocks landing only). Your own tokens pass through your own block freely. A block on a safe square is still a block. HOME ENTRY: exact count required. From home-column cell k (1..5) you need exactly 6-k to reach HOME; from p=50 you need exactly 6. Overshoot is illegal — that token cannot move and you must move a different token. NO LEGAL MOVE: if no token can use the rolled number, the turn passes (a 6 still grants its re-roll before passing, subject to the three-six cap). WINNING: first to get all 4 tokens HOME wins; play continues for 2nd/3rd and ends when only one player remains. PLAYER COUNTS: 2 players use diagonally opposite colours (red+yellow) so start offsets are 26 apart; 3 players use three of the four colours; 4 use all.
HEX MODE (5-8 seats) — this is a designed generalization, not a measurement: hexagon of 6 sectors, each sector a 3-wide x 4-long band contributing 9 ring cells, ring = 54, home column 4 cells, branch at p=52, total travel 57 pips — deliberately tuned to match the 4-player game's 56 so a 6-seat game is not 40% longer. Start offsets 9 apart. Safe squares: each sector's start cell plus one star at the sector midpoint (12 total). Sixth and fifth colours are purple #B529FE and orange #F6911B. QUICK MODE (recommended default at 5+ seats): 2 tokens per player instead of 4, and each player begins with one token already on its start square — cuts a 6-seat game from ~35 min to ~12 min.

## One-phone adaptation

HIDDEN INFORMATION: there is NONE. Every die roll and every token position in Ludo is public by definition. This is the single most important fact for the adaptation — there is NO pass-the-phone step, NO privacy screen, and NO peek/hide flow. The phone lies FLAT on the table for the entire game and is never picked up. Say this to players explicitly on the setup screen ("put the phone down in the middle — you never have to pick it up") because it is the game's biggest advantage over most party games.
SEATING & ORIENTATION: the Ludo board is radially symmetric, so the playfield itself reads correctly from every side and must NOT be rotated per player. Only the per-player text and controls rotate. Compute each seat's angle from the board's sector bisector (4-seat: the four diagonals at 45/135/225/315 degrees; hex: every 60 degrees). Anchor each seat's plate by ray-casting from the canvas centre along that angle to the canvas rectangle edge (pure math on canvas.width/height — no getBoundingClientRect), inset 10px, and rotate the plate by angle+90 degrees so its "up" points off-screen away from the board. A player sitting at the top of the portrait phone therefore reads text that is upside-down to a player at the bottom, which is exactly right. For 2 players the two seats resolve to the top and bottom edges (across the table). For 5-6 seats, the left/right rays hit the narrow side edges — render those two plates as narrow vertical pills (name only, 90/270-degree rotation) and shrink the board to 78% of width so they fit. Ludo King's own 6-player mode does precisely this with rotated name tabs, so it is proven ergonomics.
INPUT ZONES: turns are strictly sequential, so only ONE control set is ever live and zones physically cannot collide. All input is hard-locked except the active player's two affordances. (1) THE DICE PUCK — an 88px circle that slides along the board rim to sit directly between the board edge and the active player's seat plate, rotated to face them. It is always within that player's arm's reach and never under a neighbour's hand. Tap to roll. (2) TOKEN SELECTION — after the roll, legal tokens get a pulsing halo plus a small chevron oriented toward the active seat; tap the token. Hit-testing uses a 1.6x-cell radius with nearest-legal-token resolution, because at 390px width a 15x15 board gives ~23px cells, well under the 44px touch minimum. REACH FALLBACK (essential — a player at the top edge cannot comfortably reach a token near the bottom edge): the seat plate doubles as a cycler. Tap the plate to cycle the selection through legal tokens (selected token gets a bright ring and a ghost preview of its destination cell drawn as a dashed outline); the dice puck then turns into a green check — tap it to commit. Every move is therefore completable using only the two controls sitting at your own edge. AUTO-MOVE: if exactly one legal move exists, execute it automatically after a 400ms preview — this removes the majority of reach problems outright. ANTI-MISTAP: 250ms input arming delay after each turn handoff, and rolls only register if the touch starts inside the puck.
TURN COMMUNICATION (flat phone, six people, no one holding it): four redundant simultaneous cues — the board's outer glow ring takes the active colour, the active seat plate scales up and gains a pulsing gold glow while all others desaturate to 45% alpha, a gold chevron on the board rim points outward at the live seat, and a short WebAudio blip (procedurally synthesised, no assets) plus vibration if available.
7-8 PLAYERS: the board caps at 6 sectors, so seats 7 and 8 are DUOS. With 7 players one colour is shared by two people; with 8, two colours are shared. Duo partners alternate turns on their shared colour and win together; each SEAT still gets identical turn frequency, so the game stays fair between teams. The shared seat plate shows both names with the currently-acting partner highlighted.
SETUP FLOW: a top-down phone diagram with N tappable seat rings around it; each player taps their physical seat position, types a name in a keyboard panel rotated to face them, and the colour is assigned by sector. Then "Everyone ready" and the board appears — no further chrome.

## Drawing it with no assets

Zero assets — everything is canvas 2D paths, gradients and procedural noise. BOARD (4-seat): board = min(W,H)*0.90 (or *0.78 when 5+ seats need side plates); cell = board/15; origin centred. Draw a white rounded-rect base (radius cell*0.3) with a 3px #2A3340 stroke. Yards: four fillRect(6*cell) in the seat colour; inside each, a white rounded rect spanning cells 1-4 (radius cell*0.25) with a 1.5px #C9D2DC stroke; four token slots as arc() circles r=cell*0.42, filled with a createRadialGradient offset to the upper-left going lighten(colour,30%) -> colour -> darken(colour,22%), plus a 2px darker ring. Home columns and start cells: plain fillRect in the seat colour. Grid: one pass of strokeRect over every cell of the four arms, lineWidth = max(1, cell*0.035), #2A3340. Centre 3x3: four triangles via moveTo(corner)/lineTo(corner)/lineTo(exact centre)/fill, one per colour. STARS: procedural 10-vertex path alternating rOuter=cell*0.36 / rInner=cell*0.15 starting at -PI/2, stroke only, lineWidth cell*0.06, lineJoin='round', #4A5560. ARROWS: three round-capped strokes (shaft + two 40-degree head lines) in the seat colour. HEX BOARD: regular hexagon radius R; for each of 6 wedges compute the two bounding rays, then generate each cell as a quad by bilinear lerp between innerRadius/outerRadius and the two rays — moveTo/lineTo/lineTo/lineTo/closePath, fill then stroke. Centre hexagon filled #08131A with a subtle radial gradient to #0F2530. TOKEN (the map pin, r = cell*0.40): first an ellipse base ring — ctx.ellipse(0, 0.9r, 1.05r, 0.38r) filled with mix(colour,'#4A1418',0.55); then the pin body — ctx.arc(0,-1.35r, r, PI*0.82, PI*0.18) then two quadraticCurveTo from the arc endpoints to the tip at (0, 0.85r), filled with a linearGradient(-r,-2r -> r,0) from #FFFFFF to #C9CFD6, stroked 2px #6C7480; then the lens — arc(0,-1.35r, 0.58r) filled with a radialGradient offset up-left from lighten(colour,35%) to colour to darken(colour,25%); then a specular ellipse at (-0.2r, -1.62r) rx 0.24r ry 0.14r in rgba(255,255,255,0.55). Shadow: draw a manual rgba(0,0,0,0.20) ellipse at (0, 1.0r) BEFORE the token rather than relying on ctx.shadow* (and never ctx.filter, which is banned). DIE: manual rounded square via arcTo, side = cell*2.2, radius side*0.22, fill linearGradient #FFFFFF -> #E7ECF2, 3px #B7C0CB stroke; pips are arc() r = side*0.09 in #14335C on the classic 3x3 lattice. BACKGROUND: render once at init into an offscreen surface and blit each frame — use `new OffscreenCanvas(W,H)` where supported, otherwise a SECOND canvas element declared in the initial HTML source (document.createElement is banned, so it must be authored in markup, not created). Contents: a createRadialGradient from centre #4A88EA to edge #0E3E7E, then a tiled diagonal wallpaper of rounded rects rotated 30 degrees on a 120px lattice in rgba(20,26,60,0.30), then a procedural speckle pass — a seeded xorshift PRNG placing ~4000 1-2px rgba(255,255,255,0.03) dots for paper grain. PANELS: a single roundRectPanel(x,y,w,h,r) helper doing gold border + dark gold outer stroke + navy vertical gradient fill + top inner highlight + manual offset shadow rect. ROTATED TEXT: ctx.save(); ctx.translate(anchorX,anchorY); ctx.rotate(seatAngle + PI/2); draw plate and text at local origin; ctx.restore(). CONFETTI/BURSTS: arrays of {x,y,vx,vy,rot,life} drawn as 3-point filled triangles with gravity — no sprites. FONTS: link Cormorant/Bebas/Nunito via a Google Fonts <link> in the HTML head (the one permitted remote host); always give fallback stacks ('Bebas Neue', Impact, sans-serif).

## Risks

- Cell size collapse: 15x15 cells at 390px width gives ~23px cells and ~19px tokens — below the 44px touch target. Mitigate with 1.6x-cell hit radius + nearest-legal resolution, auto-move on single legal move, and the seat-plate cycler as a full alternative input path. Test at 360px width.
- Physical reach across a flat phone: a player at the top edge genuinely cannot tap a token at the bottom edge without leaning over the board. The seat-plate cycler + confirm-on-puck flow is not optional polish — it is the primary input path for far tokens and must ship in v1.
- Game length blowout: 6 players x 4 tokens with captures can run 35+ minutes and party games die at 20. Ship Quick Mode (2 tokens, one pre-deployed) as the DEFAULT for 5+ seats, and add a per-turn soft timer with auto-move on expiry.
- Rotated text legibility: Bebas Neue below ~13px read upside-down across a table becomes guesswork. Keep rotated content to name + 2 status glyphs only, never body copy, and never rotate the roll history or rules text.
- Hex-board geometry is a generalization, not a measurement — I confirmed the 4-player 15x15 grid cell-by-cell from a real screenshot, but the 6-sector ring length, home-column length and safe-square placement are my design choices tuned for equal game length. Do not present them as authentic Ludo King numbers.
- Rule ambiguity will cause table arguments: the blockade rule (block landing only vs. block passage), whether an unusable 6 grants a re-roll, and the exact three-sixes penalty all differ between sources. Pick the stated defaults, expose them as toggles in setup, and show the active ruleset on a one-tap info panel so the table can settle disputes without leaving the game.
