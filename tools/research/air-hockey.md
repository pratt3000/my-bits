# air-hockey — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

What I actually saw in the Glow Hockey screenshot: a pure black void playfield with no table texture at all, framed by neon "tube" rails where every stroke is a blinding white core wrapped in a coloured bloom — the top-left rail bloomed red, top-right yellow, bottom-left blue, bottom-right green, so each quadrant of the frame is a different hue and the goal is simply a gap in the tube with a short white cap-tick at each edge. The mallets are not solid discs: they are thick white annuli (a fat white ring) with a dark coloured hub in the middle and a soft coloured halo outside — green for the far player, red for the near player. The puck is a plain white disc followed by a trail of 6-10 ghosted grey-blue circles that shrink and fade backwards along its path. Centre line and centre circle are hairline white at roughly 30% alpha with a wide faint under-glow, plus a scatter of tiny blue star-specks drifting in the middle third. In the Neon Hockey shot the arena was an octagonal chamfered ring — magenta tubes for the far half, aqua for the near half, an amber faceted puck dragging a solid tapering wedge trail, and goal bursts drawn as white-hot five-point stars with green and cyan sparks. The physical arcade tables I looked at contribute one thing only: the perforated air-hole lattice (a faint regular dot grid) and magenta LED underglow. The whole identity is: black, additive light, white cores, saturated blooms, zero fills, zero gradients-as-material.

## Palette

- `#04060B` — void background base fill, the whole screen before anything else
- `#0B1130` — arena-centre radial vignette (createRadialGradient to transparent), the only non-black ground tone
- `#FFFFFF` — neon hot core — the innermost 2.5px pass of EVERY stroke, the puck body, the mallet ring, all text fills
- `#FF2D55` — P1 red-pink rail / mallet halo / hub
- `#3FE7FF` — P2 cyan rail + all shared HUD chrome (pause ring, countdown chips) as in the reference
- `#3BFF6B` — P3 neon green rail; also the 'READY / GO' overlay word colour
- `#FFD629` — P4 amber rail; also the alternate puck tint in Neon Hockey mode
- `#C86BFF` — P5 violet rail (matches the magenta tube I saw in Neon Hockey)
- `#FF7A18` — P6 orange rail
- `#4D7BFF` — P7 blue rail
- `#FF4FD8` — P8 magenta rail
- `#4A5570` — dim markings — centre line, centre circle, goal creases, sealed/eliminated walls, dot-lattice air holes (use at 0.25-0.35 alpha)

## Typography

Bebas Neue is the primary and carries ~90% of the type. The lettering in the Glow Hockey settings screen I viewed ("SETTINGS", "CHANGE PADDLES", "CLASSIC") is uppercase-only, narrow, squared-off, flat-terminalled arcade neon — Bebas Neue is the only approved face with that tall condensed all-caps proportion, and condensed matters enormously here because rotated HUD capsules for 8 players are physically tiny. Use it for score digits, the big GOAL / P3 WINS / 3-2-1 overlays, player labels, and buttons. Space Mono is the secondary, used ONLY for numerics that tick: the 7-second possession countdown and the lives/score chips — tabular mono digits do not jitter their width as they count down, which a proportional face would. Press Start 2P is explicitly restricted to a single optional use: the small "AIR HOCKEY" title wordmark on the front screen. Do not use it anywhere in the HUD — its fixed square em is unreadable below ~16px and catastrophic when rotated into an 8-player edge capsule. Render all text with the neon recipe: strokeText at lineWidth 8 in rgba(colour,0.10), strokeText at lineWidth 4 in rgba(colour,0.45), then fillText in #FFFFFF. Always declare fallbacks ("Bebas Neue", "Arial Narrow", system-ui, sans-serif) and lay out from ctx.measureText, never from assumed metrics.

## Shape language

Everything is a circle, a straight tube, or a rounded corner — there is not one sharp filled shape in the references. Rails are constant-width round-capped, round-joined strokes; the Duel rink is a rounded rectangle with corner radius ≈ 9% of rink width, the Dome arena is a regular N-gon with each vertex softened by a 10px arc. Goals are absences, never drawn objects: you build each wall as explicit moveTo/lineTo runs that skip the gap, then add a 10px white perpendicular post-tick at each gap edge. Puck and mallet are perfect concentric circles — puck is a filled disc, mallet is a ring (outer halo r=1.5R, white annulus stroked at lineWidth 0.42R on radius 0.72R, coloured hub disc r=0.30R). Field markings are pure geometry: one hairline centre line, one centre circle at r ≈ 0.28 rink width, one semicircular goal crease arc per goal. Particles are 5-point stars (10-vertex path alternating r and 0.42r) and small circles — nothing else. Zero rectangles with fills, zero bevels, zero drop shadows in the material sense. The only filled area in the whole game is the background and the translucent panel behind a modal (rgba(6,8,18,0.72) rounded rect with a neon tube border, exactly as the settings card I saw).

## UI chrome

Copy the reference literally. Pause is a circled double-bar: a stroked circle r=17 with two 3px rounded vertical bars inside, cyan neon, sitting in a screen corner — place TWO of them, top-left and bottom-right, so the rotational symmetry lets either end player reach one. Score lives at the player's own end, not in a shared bar: in Duel, a Bebas digit ~74px tall centred on each player's end wall in that player's colour, drawn under a ctx.rotate for the top player so it reads upright from their seat (the reference puts them along the right edge rotated 90°; end-wall placement is the same idea done better for a phone flat on a table). In Dome, each player gets a small rotated capsule OUTSIDE their wall, between the wall and the screen edge: a stroked rounded-rect 62x22 in their colour containing three life pips (filled circles r=3.5, spent ones drop to 0.15 alpha) — no words, no numbers, there is no room at N=8. Modal panels: rounded rect, rgba(6,8,18,0.72) fill, bordered with the 4-pass neon recipe in red or cyan, contents in white-cored Bebas; toggles are stroked pills with a filled cyan knob; nav arrows are filled triangles at 0.85 alpha with a matching halo — all exactly as in the settings screen I viewed. Every modal renders its text twice minimum (0 rad and PI rad, mirrored across screen centre) and in Dome renders a small rotated echo per seated player. Buttons get a 1px inner white rim plus outer bloom and brighten 25% on pointerdown with a 90ms ease.

## Motion

Zero-latency and violently additive. Fixed-timestep accumulator at 1/120s physics with interpolated render, dt capped at 0.05s. The mallet must feel welded to the finger: one frame of smoothing at most (target lerp 0.55) plus a hard speed clamp of 2400 px/s, never an easing curve — latency is the entire game. Puck trail length scales with speed: trailCount = clamp(floor(speed/180), 2, 12); each ghost i drawn in 'lighter' at radius R*(0.35+0.65*i/n) and alpha 0.03+0.10*i/n. On wall impact the puck squashes 18% along the impact normal and 12% across it for 90ms with an overshooting return, applied via save/translate/rotate(normalAngle)/scale. Hit-stop: 20ms freeze on any mallet strike above 1600 px/s, 55ms on a goal — the single cheapest piece of juice available and it transforms the feel. Goal sequence: hit-stop, then a full-screen fillRect flash in the conceding wall's colour at 0.22 alpha decaying over 250ms, then screen shake amp 7px for 260ms as amp*(1-t)*sin(t*58) applied by translating the play layer only (background painted full-bleed first so the shake never exposes the void edge), then an expanding ring at the goal mouth from r=0 to 220px with lineWidth easing 14 to 0 over 450ms, plus 14 star particles and 22 sparks at 300-900 px/s with drag 0.94 and 0.55s life, no gravity. Idle: every rail's core alpha hums at 0.88+0.12*sin(t*2.1+wallIndex) so the neon breathes; the conceding wall ramps its bloom to full white and back over 400ms. Countdown digits scale 1.6 to 1.0 with alpha 0 to 1 over 180ms, hold, then pop to 1.25 scale and alpha 0 in 120ms.

## Mechanics

RULES AS RESEARCHED (USAA / standard). Game is first to 7 goals, flat — there is no win-by-two in the official rules. Match option: best of 3 games to 7. Start: coin toss or centre face-off decides first possession; puck is placed dead centre and is live for either player. CENTRELINE: a player may strike the puck only while it is on their own half OR touching the centreline; a mallet that fully crosses the centreline is a foul and loses possession. A puck sitting on the centreline is live for both and constitutes possession for neither. 7-SECOND RULE: once the puck enters and remains on your side you have 7 seconds to send a shot across the centreline, else foul and turnover. 10-SECOND RULE: after conceding you have 10 seconds to retrieve the puck and restart. POSSESSION AFTER A GOAL GOES TO THE PLAYER WHO WAS SCORED UPON. TOPPING (setting the mallet on top of the puck to trap or slow it) and PALMING are fouls: turnover, or a disallowed goal. Puck contacting your hand, arm, body or clothing is a foul (exception: hand-serving after it enters your own goal). Blocking a shot with anything but the mallet is a foul. Knocking the puck off the table with your own offensive motion is a foul against you. Timeout: one 10-second timeout per player per game, callable only while you hold the puck; a second one draws a conduct warning. Physical specs for proportions: 8-foot tournament table (playfield ~90.5 x 39.5 in), puck 3.25 in diameter, mallet 3.75 in typical with a USAA cap of 4-1/16 in and 6 oz, goal mouth 12-16 in. So puck radius ≈ 0.041 of playfield width, mallet ≈ 1.15x the puck, goal ≈ 0.30-0.40 of width.
DIGITAL TRANSLATION — enforce by geometry, never by whistle. Clamp the mallet centre so it can touch but not cross the line (y >= centreY + Rm), which makes the centreline foul and the strike-legality rule structurally impossible to break. Fouls that remain meaningful: (a) 7-second stall — run a per-side timer while the puck is wholly on that side; from 4.0s draw a Space Mono countdown ring around the puck; at 0 the puck teleports to the opponent's face-off spot with them serving. (b) Anti-pin, the digital analogue of topping — if puck speed < 30 px/s for 1.5s while in mallet contact, apply an automatic 260 px/s impulse along the contact normal away from the mallet. (c) Global anti-stall — puck speed < 20 px/s anywhere for 3s spawns a puff ring and a 120 px/s impulse toward centre. Serve: puck respawns frozen at the conceding player's face-off spot for 1.2s, then goes live with zero velocity; it stays inert until that player's mallet touches it or 3s elapse.
PHYSICS, EXACT. Puck: radius Rp = 0.045 * rinkWidth; mallet Rm = 1.35 * Rp (bigger than real, needed for touch). Goal mouth = 0.34 * rinkWidth. Air-cushion friction v *= pow(0.995, dt*60) — roughly 26% speed loss per second. Wall restitution e = 0.92. Puck speed clamp 2600 px/s, mallet clamp 2400 px/s. Mallet-puck resolution treats the mallet as infinite mass: n = normalize(pPuck - pMallet); push the puck out to exactly Rp+Rm along n; vRel = vPuck - vMallet; vn = dot(vRel, n); if vn < 0 then vPuck = vPuck - (1+0.95)*vn*n + vMallet*0.55 + n*40. The +vMallet*0.55 term is what makes a fast swipe genuinely accelerate the puck; the +n*40 minimum separation impulse is what stops a resting puck sticking to the mallet. Mallet velocity is derived from actual per-frame displacement, not from raw finger delta. SUBSTEPPING IS MANDATORY: at 2600 px/s and 60fps the puck advances ~43px while its radius is ~18px, so it tunnels through rails and through goal posts. Split each frame into ceil(speed*dt / (Rp*0.5)) substeps capped at 8, and test the goal plane on every substep. Goal is scored when the puck centre passes the wall line while its lateral offset is within the gap half-width.
DOME MODE RULES (3-8 players). Regular N-gon; player i owns the edge whose outward normal points at angle PI/2 + i*2PI/N in screen coords, so player 0's wall faces the bottom of the phone. Goal is a centred gap spanning goalFrac = 0.34 + 0.02*N of that edge (it must widen with N or 8-player games end in seconds). Each player starts with 3 lives; conceding costs 1; at 0 you are eliminated — your gap seals into a solid dim #4A5570 segment, your mallet is removed, your HUD drops to 25% alpha. Last player alive wins. Track lastToucherId; if the wall owner was the last toucher, show OWN GOAL in their colour — the life is still lost. After each goal: 1.0s freeze, respawn at the arena centre with a 700 px/s impulse in a random direction, 1.5s countdown. Stall breaker: if 45s pass with no goal, shrink the arena radius 0.6% per second until someone scores.
TEAMS VARIANT (even N, rect rink): 2v2 / 3v3 / 4v4 on the Duel rink, up to 4 mallets per half, shared score, first to 7.

## One-phone adaptation

Air hockey has ZERO hidden information, so there is no pass-the-phone privacy screen and no turn passing anywhere in this game — every player acts simultaneously and continuously. The phone lies FLAT on a table, portrait, and stays put; nobody picks it up. The entire adaptation budget goes into seating orientation, non-overlapping multitouch zones, and keeping fingers off the puck.
TWO PLAYERS — DUEL. The two players face each other across the SHORT edges (top and bottom of the portrait screen), mirroring a real table's long axis. Hard input split at y = H/2: a touch whose touchstart lands in the top half belongs to the top player, bottom half to the bottom player. First touch per half wins; any additional touch starting in the same half is ignored, which prevents one person grabbing two mallets. Full 2-D drag within your half. Because the mallet is clamped to its own half, dragging your finger past the centreline simply pins your mallet on the line — which is exactly the real rule, enforced for free. The top player's score digit, name, 7-second countdown and every overlay word are drawn under ctx.rotate(PI) so they read upright from their seat.
THREE TO EIGHT — DOME. A regular N-gon arena centred in the portrait screen, inscribed radius r = min(W,H)/2 - 16 (about 179px on a 390px-wide phone). Each player owns one edge, positioned at their real seat angle around the phone: player 0 at the bottom, then counter-clockwise. The screen is partitioned into N angular wedges measured from the arena centre — wedge boundaries sit on the polygon vertices, so the wedges tile the entire plane including the screen corners, with zero overlap and zero gaps. A touch belongs to the player whose wedge contains atan2(ty-cy, tx-cx) at touchstart.
The critical move: the mallet is 1-D, sliding along a track parallel to the player's own edge, offset inward by Rm+6 and clamped to the edge extent minus Rm. Your finger's position is PROJECTED onto that track axis, so you can control your mallet from anywhere in your wedge — including the dead space outside the arena, right at the screen border nearest you. With 8 people around a 390px phone, fingers absolutely will occlude the playfield unless they are kept off it, so draw a persistent "grip bar" — a 90x8 rounded neon bar in the player's colour at the screen edge inside their wedge — telling each person where to rest their thumb. This single decision is what makes 8-player play physically possible. (2-D-within-wedge control is available as a variant for N=3 and N=4, but keep 1-D as the default for fairness across counts.)
HUD ROTATION: each Dome player's life capsule sits in the dead space just outside their wall, rotated by (their wall normal angle - PI/2) so it reads from their seat. Overlays never render once: GOAL, the 3-2-1 countdown and the winner banner are drawn N times, once per seat, each rotated to that player's angle and positioned near their wall — plus one large centre copy for spectators.
READY GATE: before the opening serve, every player must hold a finger in their own wedge. Each wedge shows a rotated ring meter filling over 0.8s while held, plus their colour name; the round starts only when all N rings are full. This assigns colours, confirms seating, and guarantees nobody is caught unprepared by the first face-off.
PAUSE: two rotationally symmetric pause buttons (top-left and bottom-right) so anyone can reach one; the pause card renders its text at 0 and PI plus small rotated echoes per seat.

## Drawing it with no assets

Everything is canvas 2D primitives. NO ctx.filter (banned and unnecessary), NO document.createElement, NO getBoundingClientRect.
GLOW — the core recipe, used for every luminous element. Draw the same path 4 times with ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter', lineCap and lineJoin = 'round': pass 1 lineWidth 18 at rgba(colour, 0.06); pass 2 lineWidth 11 at rgba(colour, 0.12); pass 3 lineWidth 6 at rgba(colour, 0.55); pass 4 lineWidth 2.5 in solid #FFFFFF. That white core inside a coloured bloom is precisely what I saw on every rail, mallet ring and letterform. ctx.shadowBlur/shadowColor is legal (it is not the filter property) and can be added on pass 3 at shadowBlur 24 for extra bloom, but gate it behind an adaptive quality flag — see risks.
COORDINATES WITHOUT getBoundingClientRect: make the canvas fill the viewport at position:fixed, left:0, top:0, width:100vw, height:100vh, touch-action:none. Set canvas.width = innerWidth*dpr and canvas.height = innerHeight*dpr, then ctx.setTransform(dpr,0,0,dpr,0,0) once. Touch clientX/clientY then map directly to CSS-pixel canvas coords with no rect lookup at all.
BACKGROUND: fillRect the whole screen #04060B, then a createRadialGradient from the arena centre, r=0 to r=maxDim*0.7, #0B1130 at 0.35 alpha to transparent. Starfield: 70 points from a seeded mulberry32 PRNG, r 0.6-1.6px, base alpha 0.15-0.6, twinkled as alpha*(0.7+0.3*sin(t*speed+phase)) — matches the blue star-specks in the reference.
AIR-HOLE LATTICE: a 22px-pitch dot grid at rgba(74,85,112,0.30), r=1.0, clipped to the arena path — the only nod to the physical table. Batch it as ONE beginPath with many moveTo+arc calls then a single fill; ~600 dots costs one draw call. To cache it, use new OffscreenCanvas(w,h) — a constructor, not document.createElement, so it is legal — with a feature check and the per-frame batched fallback.
RAILS: build each wall as explicit moveTo/lineTo runs that skip the goal gap, then run the 4-pass glow. Add a 10px white perpendicular post-tick at each gap edge. Rounded corners via arcTo (Duel) or a 10px arc at each N-gon vertex (Dome). Sealed/eliminated walls draw as a single flat stroke in #4A5570 with no bloom.
PUCK: createRadialGradient(x-Rp*0.3, y-Rp*0.3, 0, x, y, Rp) from #FFFFFF to the puck tint, filled; then an outer 'lighter' ring stroke. Trail from a ring buffer of the last 12 positions as described in motion, or as a tapered wedge — a filled quad between p[n-1] +/- perp*Rp and p[0] +/- perp*Rp*0.15 with a createLinearGradient along the path — to reproduce the solid trail I saw in Neon Hockey.
MALLET, four layers: (a) 'lighter' filled circle r=1.5*Rm with a radial gradient from rgba(colour,0.45) to transparent — the halo; (b) strokeStyle #FFFFFF, lineWidth 0.42*Rm, arc at r=0.72*Rm — the fat white annulus; (c) filled disc r=0.30*Rm in the player colour at 0.9 alpha — the hub; (d) a 1px rgba(255,255,255,0.9) inner rim.
MARKINGS: centre line as a 1.5px rgba(255,255,255,0.30) stroke over a 5px rgba(255,255,255,0.06) under-stroke; centre circle arc at r = 0.28*rinkWidth; goal crease semicircle at rgba(255,255,255,0.16).
PARTICLES: stars are a 10-vertex path alternating radius r and 0.42r; sparks are small filled circles. All in 'lighter'. Goal ring is an arc with lineWidth eased 14 to 0 as radius grows 0 to 220px.
TEXT: strokeText lineWidth 8 at rgba(colour,0.10), strokeText lineWidth 4 at rgba(colour,0.45), fillText #FFFFFF. Rotate with save/translate/rotate/restore; measure with ctx.measureText.
Total asset payload: zero bytes. Everything above is arithmetic.

## Risks

- TUNNELING: at the 2600 px/s clamp the puck advances ~43px per 60fps frame against an ~18px radius, so it passes straight through rails and goal posts. Substepping (ceil(speed*dt/(Rp*0.5)), capped at 8) with per-substep goal-plane tests is not optional — build it first or the game is unshippable.
- MULTITOUCH IDENTIFIER CHURN: iOS Safari reuses touch identifiers and mixing e.touches with e.changedTouches silently swaps mallets between players mid-rally. Keep a Map keyed by identifier, assign ownership ONLY on touchstart, release ONLY on touchend/touchcancel, and reconcile the map against e.touches on every event to drop stale entries. Also preventDefault on touchmove and set touch-action:none or the page scroll-bounces under 8 fingers.
- FINGER OCCLUSION AT HIGH PLAYER COUNTS: eight hands on a 390px-wide phone will physically cover the puck. The 1-D edge-track control with grip bars drawn at the screen border is the mitigation; if the implementer 'improves' it to 2-D playfield dragging for all counts, 6-8 player games become unplayable.
- GLOW COST: 8 walls x 4 stroke passes, a 12-ghost trail and 36 particles per goal will drop mid-range Android below 30fps, and ctx.shadowBlur is by far the worst offender. Default to layered strokes only; measure frame time and drop shadowBlur, then trail length, then starfield twinkle after 3 consecutive frames over 22ms.
- DOME FAIRNESS: at N=8 each polygon edge is short and games end in seconds unless the goal gap scales — use goalFrac = 0.34 + 0.02*N, and verify a 3-player game is not trivially defendable at the other extreme.
- ROTATED TEXT MUD: neon strokes on rotated Bebas Neue below ~14px turn into grey smear if the canvas is not backed at device pixels. Set canvas.width = innerWidth*dpr and ctx.setTransform(dpr,0,0,dpr,0,0) exactly once at init, and never re-scale per draw.
