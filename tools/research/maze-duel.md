# maze-duel — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

I viewed five references directly: a photo of the Ravensburger Labyrinth board (cream stone corridors, orange brick walls, a navy-and-gold frame with amber shift arrows, glossy cylindrical wooden pegs in red/yellow/blue/green), the classic seven-circuit Cretan labyrinth SVG (pure black unicursal stroke, uniform weight, every cap and turn rounded, spiralling to a centre), an aerial photo of the Schoenbrunn hedge maze (yew slabs with a bright yellow-green sunlit crown, a mid-green face, near-black crevices at the base, and warm pale gravel corridors), the Hampton Court maze plan (a single-weight hand-drawn line plan with a goal at the middle), and the Pac-Man arcade screenshot (black void, corridors exactly one cell wide, walls as thin double-outlined rounded-rect strokes in a deep royal blue I sampled at #2417A6, peach pellets at #F1B49B). The identity I am committing to fuses these: a formal hedge maze seen from directly overhead, laid inside a Ravensburger-style navy-and-gold board frame, sitting on a dark oiled-walnut tabletop. Corridors are warm gravel (#CFC3A6) with real grain; hedges are chunky rounded-corner green slabs with a lit crown along their upper-left edge and a hard dark shadow cast down-right, so the whole board reads as physical objects lit from up-left rather than as a flat diagram. The centre goal is a circular limestone plaza with the seven-circuit Cretan spiral engraved into it in pale ochre, pulsing with a thin gold ring so it is unmistakable from any side of the table. Player pawns are top-down wooden pegs, exactly the glossy cylinders I saw in the board photo: a colour disc with an offset radial gradient, a darker rim, a white specular ellipse up-left, and a soft ground shadow down-right. The overall register is warm, tactile, and slightly ceremonial — a garden, not a grid.

## Typography

Two faces only. SPACE GROTESK carries every functional element — player 3-letter tags on pawns, wall-budget pips, buttons, turn clock, seat plates. Its geometric grotesque skeleton stays legible at 10-12px, its slightly odd terminals (the flat-topped 'a', the angled 'G' spur) give it board-game personality rather than the anonymity of Inter, and it holds up when rotated 90/180 degrees for players on the far side of the table, which is the deciding factor. Set labels ALL CAPS at 0.14em tracking, sizes 10/12/14/20px, weight 500 for data and 700 for buttons. CORMORANT GARAMOND (600, italic) is reserved for exactly three ceremonial moments — the title card, the three Boon card names, and the win banner — because the high-contrast old-style serif is the voice of a baroque formal garden and of the classical labyrinth, matching what the Schoenbrunn and Cretan references actually feel like. Explicitly reject Press Start 2P: Pac-Man is a structural reference here (one-cell corridors, rounded outlined walls), not a stylistic one, and pixel type would fight the organic hedge texture. Reject Bebas Neue as too athletic and DM Serif Display as too heavy at small sizes.

## Shape language

Everything is a rounded rectangle or a circle; there is not one sharp corner on the play surface. Hedge slabs are rounded-rects with corner radius 0.32 x cell, echoing both the rounded caps of the Cretan labyrinth stroke and the clipped-topiary silhouette in the hedge photo. Corridors are therefore rounded-void channels, exactly like the black gaps between Pac-Man's outlined blue walls. Pawns are perfect circles (top-down pegs) at r = 0.30 x cell, never squares, so they never read as terrain. The Heart is the only large circle: a limestone disc of r = 1.15 x cell with the unicursal spiral inside it. The board frame is a hard-edged band with a 2px gold inner keyline and eight inward-pointing gold triangles at the compass points — the one deliberately angular motif, lifted straight from the amber shift arrows on the Ravensburger board edge, used as seat markers. Legal-move footprints are rounded-squares (radius 0.25 x cell) so they sit in the terrain grammar, never circles, so they can never be mistaken for pawns. The eight player colours are a fixed ramp chosen so that green belongs to the hedges alone and no pawn ever competes with it: crimson #C81E1E, amber #F2A50C, azure #1E6FD9, violet #8B44C7, cyan #0FB5B5, rose #E0568A, bone #F3EDE0, ink #2A2A33.

## UI chrome

All chrome is a navy plate (#24406E) with a 1.5px gold (#FFC100) inner keyline and 10px corner radius — the board frame's material, repeated. Buttons are pills, 46px tall, radius 23, filled with the ACTIVE player's colour with the label in auto-contrast Space Grotesk 700 12px caps; disabled pills go #1B2E52 with #5A6B8C text. The only persistent HUD lives in the frame itself: eight seat plates, one at each compass post, each a 62x26 rounded plate rotated so it reads right-side-up from that seat, containing a colour swatch, the 3-letter tag, and the wall budget drawn as tiny hedge-slab pips (filled = available, hollow 1px outline = spent). The active seat's plate lifts 3px, gets a full gold keyline, and the whole board frame recolours to that player's hue at 40% mix. The turn clock is not a bar — it is a 3px gold arc inscribed just inside the frame, depleting clockwise around the entire board so it is readable from every seat. There is no bottom nav; the action controls are a single floating "puck" (three 44px round buttons — RUN / HEDGE / BOON — plus a CONFIRM that only appears when a move or wall is staged) that renders in the clear band on whichever side of the screen the active player is sitting, rotated to face them. Boon cards, shown only on the setup privacy screen, are 200x280 navy cards with a gold double-rule border, the name in Cormorant Garamond italic 22px, and the rule text in Space Grotesk 12px.

## Motion

Heavy and organic, with overshoot on growth and damping on travel — a hedge is not a UI element. Pawn step: 180ms cubic-out along the orthogonal path, with a 6% hop (pawn scales 1.00 -> 1.08 -> 1.00 while its ground shadow shrinks to 0.82 and darkens at the midpoint) so it reads as lifting a wooden peg off the board and setting it down. Hedge growth on commit: the slab scales along its long axis 0.2 -> 1.0 over 380ms with a back-out ease (overshoot 1.12), while 12 leaf particles (2px arcs in #91CD53 / #B7DC72) pop upward at 40-90px/s and fall under gravity 380px/s^2 over 500ms. Legal-move footprints spring in 0 -> 1 over 160ms, staggered 25ms per cell outward from the pawn. Illegal action: 120ms shake of +/-3px at 18Hz plus the ghost slab's outline flips to #C81E1E with four diagonal hatch strokes. Turn handoff: a 420ms gold arc sweeps around the frame from the old seat to the new one on ease-in-out, then the new seat plate pulses twice. Goal pulse: the Heart's gold ring oscillates alpha 0.25 -> 0.70 at 0.6Hz continuously, all game. Win: the Cretan spiral ignites ring by ring from centre outward over 900ms in the winner's colour, 60 spark/leaf particles, then the frame floods with the winner's hue over 300ms and the Cormorant banner drops in. Nothing exceeds 900ms; every interactive response starts within 1 frame of touch.

## Mechanics

CORE: "Race-to-centre maze duel" is not a published title — it is a synthesis, so the ruleset is specified exactly, built on Quoridor's verified wall rules (2-player: 10 fences each; 4-player: 5 each; a fence spans two squares; a fence may never cut off the only remaining path of ANY pawn to its goal; face-to-face pawns may be jumped straight over, and if a wall or edge blocks the landing square the mover may instead step to either square beside the jumped pawn) plus the Ravensburger Labyrinth race-and-reshape feeling and a single central goal.

BOARD: 9x9 cells, indices (c,r) from 0..8. Centre cell (4,4) is THE HEART. Wall lattice: 8x8 = 64 lattice points, index (c,r) with 0<=c,r<=7. A HORIZONTAL wall at (c,r) blocks the edges (c,r)|(c,r+1) and (c+1,r)|(c+1,r+1). A VERTICAL wall at (c,r) blocks (c,r)|(c+1,r) and (c,r+1)|(c+1,r+1). Each lattice point holds at most ONE wall — this single constraint enforces both "no overlap" and "no crossing".

SEEDED MAZE: at setup, generate a randomised-DFS perfect maze on the 9x9 grid, then delete ~45% of its interior walls so the maze is loopy and has no dead-end frustration. Enforce 4-fold rotational symmetry (the true symmetry group of a square grid): generate one quadrant and rotate it 3 times, so every seat faces an identical puzzle. Seeded walls occupy roughly 22 of the 64 lattice points and are permanent.

START CELLS: eight seats sit on the Manhattan-distance-4 ring around (4,4): N(4,0), NE(6,2), E(8,4), SE(6,6), S(4,8), SW(2,6), W(0,4), NW(2,2). For P players the seats used are slot = round(i*8/P) mod 8 for i in 0..P-1 (2p = N,S; 4p = N,E,S,W; 8p = all).

FAIRNESS GUARANTEE: after generating the maze, BFS from the Heart across open edges. ACCEPT the maze only if all P start cells have IDENTICAL shortest-path length to the Heart, and that length equals the target: 17 for 2p, 15 for 3p, 14 for 4p, 12 for 5p, 11 for 6p, 9 for 7p, 8 for 8p (+/-1 tolerance). Otherwise regenerate; cap at 200 attempts, then relax to "all within 1 of each other". This removes the geometric unfairness that axis seats have exactly one shortest path while diagonal seats have C(4,2)=6.

TURN: strict clockwise seat order (the compass spins at setup to pick the first seat). On your turn do EXACTLY ONE of:
(a) RUN — move your pawn S steps, where S = 1 if P<=4 and S = 2 if P>=5, plus +1 if you hold the Torch. Each step is one orthogonal move to an adjacent cell not separated by a wall. In a multi-step run you may not re-enter the cell you started the turn on, and you may stop early. Diagonal movement is never legal except via the jump sidestep below.
(b) HEDGE — place one wall from your budget at any legal lattice point anywhere on the board. This uses your whole turn; you do not also move.
(c) BOON — spend your one secret Boon (see below).
You may not pass voluntarily.

JUMP (per step, exactly): if the cell you would step into holds a pawn, and the cell directly beyond it in the same direction is on-board, empty, and not separated from the blocking pawn by a wall, you MAY jump to it (this counts as one step). If that beyond-cell is off-board, occupied, or wall-blocked, you may instead step DIAGONALLY to either cell orthogonally adjacent to the blocking pawn and perpendicular to your approach, provided that cell is on-board, empty, and not separated from the blocking pawn by a wall. Chained jumps over two pawns in a row are NOT allowed. Two pawns may never share a cell.

WALL LEGALITY (all three must hold): (1) the lattice point is empty (no seeded wall, no placed wall, of either orientation); (2) neither of the two half-edges it would block is already blocked by a seeded or placed wall of the same orientation; (3) after placement, EVERY pawn on the board still has at least one path to the Heart (run BFS from the Heart, check all P pawns). Precompute the full legal-slot set once per turn (64 slots x P BFS over 81 cells is ~50k ops — trivial) and cache it.

WALL BUDGET: 7 each at 2p, 5 each at 3-4p, 4 each at 5-6p, 3 each at 7-8p. Walls are permanent once placed (except via Shears).

THE TORCH (catch-up, deterministic): after every turn resolves, BFS from the Heart and compute each pawn's shortest legal path length. The single player with the LARGEST distance holds the Torch (ties broken by earliest seat slot). The Torch holder gets +1 step on a RUN. Exactly one holder at all times; the pawn is ringed in #FFE28A.

BOONS (hidden information, one per player, chosen secretly at setup, one-shot, discarded on use): VAULT — as your whole turn, step one cell THROUGH a hedge as though it were not there; the destination must be on-board and empty. SHEARS — as your whole turn, permanently destroy any one PLAYER-PLACED wall (never a seeded wall, which preserves the symmetry/fairness guarantee); it is removed from the game, not returned to its owner. QUICKSILVER — immediately after your turn ends, take one additional full turn; cannot chain into a third.

WIN: the first pawn to ENTER the Heart cell (4,4) wins immediately. Because turns are strictly sequential there are no ties. The Heart cannot be reached diagonally.

EDGE CASES: (i) Walling all four edges of the Heart is automatically illegal under rule (3), so the goal can never be sealed; walling three of four is legal and is the game's central tension. (ii) If a player has no legal run, no walls left, and no unused Boon, their turn is auto-skipped with a "BLOCKED" stamp; rule (3) guarantees this is transient (caused only by pawn positions). (iii) If a player has walls but no legal run, they must place a wall. (iv) TURN CLOCK: 25s at P<=4, 15s at P>=5; on expiry the pawn auto-runs one step along its current shortest path to the Heart, or is skipped if it has none.

## One-phone adaptation

THE KEY INSIGHT: a top-down maze is orientation-neutral. Unlike cards or chess, the board does NOT need re-orienting per seat — a hedge maze is equally valid viewed from any side. So the phone lies FLAT on the table for the entire game and is never passed during play. Only symbolic overlays (names, numerals, buttons) get rotated.

LAYOUT (portrait, e.g. 390x844): the board is a square of side = min(vw - 24, vh - 300), centred horizontally, positioned so there is a clear ~140px band above and ~150px below. The eight compass seat posts sit ON the board frame at N/NE/E/SE/S/SW/W/NW; each seat plate is drawn with ctx.rotate so its text faces outward toward that physical seat (N plate rotated 180deg, E plate rotated -90deg, NE plate rotated 135deg, etc.). Players physically sit at the compass point matching their in-game start, so their pawn is always the one nearest their own hands.

INPUT — direct manipulation on the board, orientation-free: only one player acts at a time and the board has no "up", so the whole core loop is board-local taps that work identically from every side. RUN mode: tap your own pawn to arm it (it lifts, its ground shadow shrinks, and every legal destination springs in as a rounded-square footprint); tap a lit footprint to move, tap your pawn again to cancel. Drag-to-move is also accepted with a 22px snap. HEDGE mode: tap any gap between two cells; the nearest legal lattice slot within 22px is snapped to and a translucent ghost hedge appears; tap the small gold rotate handle on the ghost to flip H/V; tap CONFIRM to commit, tap anywhere else to cancel. An illegal slot shows the red hatched ghost and never commits.

MODE SWITCH SOLVES TAP COLLISION: at 9x9 the cells are ~36px and the wall gaps between them are only ~4px of true geometry — far too small to disambiguate. The RUN/HEDGE mode toggle means only ONE target class is live at a time, so in RUN mode the whole 36px cell is a target and in HEDGE mode the whole 36px cell area maps to its nearest lattice slot. This is the single most important interaction decision.

THE FLOATING PUCK — the only rotated chrome: exactly one control cluster on screen, rendered in the clear band on the side nearest the ACTIVE player and rotated to face them. Seats N/NE/NW -> top band, rotated 180deg. Seats S/SE/SW -> bottom band, upright. Seat E -> right edge as a vertical stack rotated -90deg. Seat W -> left edge rotated +90deg. It holds three 44px round buttons (RUN / HEDGE / BOON) plus a CONFIRM pill that appears only when something is staged. Because there is never more than one puck, input zones can never overlap between players.

TURN PASSING: no handoff, no privacy screen, no "ready?" tap during play. A 420ms gold arc sweeps around the board frame from the previous seat to the next, the new seat plate lifts and pulses twice, the frame tints to the new player's colour, and the puck slides to their side. Total dead time between turns ~0.5s.

HIDDEN INFORMATION — one privacy pass, at setup only: the ONLY secret is each player's chosen Boon. Doing a privacy screen every turn with 8 players would be ruinous, so it is compressed into a single setup pass. Sequentially, a full-screen cover in each player's colour reads "PASS TO ARI" in Cormorant italic, rotated to face that seat; they PRESS AND HOLD to reveal the three Boon cards (also rotated to their seat), tap one, and the screen instantly re-covers on release. Roughly 8-10 seconds per player, once, then the phone goes flat and stays there. A group that prefers zero handoffs can flip an "OPEN BOONS" toggle at setup, which shows everyone's Boon publicly on their seat plate.

SETUP FLOW: (1) "HOW MANY WALK THE MAZE?" — chips 2..8. (2) Seat claiming — the board renders with the P computed compass posts glowing; each player taps their own post, then picks a colour from the 8-ramp and types a 3-letter tag on a keypad rotated to face their post. (3) The Boon privacy pass. (4) The compass spins for ~1.2s and lands on the first seat.

DOWNTIME MANAGEMENT AT HIGH COUNTS: 8 players means 7 turns of waiting, so runs become 2 steps at P>=5, the clock drops to 15s, moves are a single tap, and the target race length shortens to 8 steps — putting an 8-player game at roughly 5 minutes and ~100 fast turns.

## Drawing it with no assets

ZERO ASSETS. Everything below is canvas 2D primitives, gradients, seeded procedural noise, or an inline SVG path string turned into a Path2D. No images, no remote URLs, no emoji, no createElement, no getBoundingClientRect (size from window.innerWidth/innerHeight and canvas.width/height; hit-test in canvas coords derived from touch clientX/Y minus a known fixed layout origin), no ctx.filter.

THREE-LAYER RENDER. (L1) STATIC BOARD — baked once into an OffscreenCanvas (new OffscreenCanvas(w,h), which is not createElement; fallback: build a persistent ImageData via new ImageData(w,h) and putImageData). (L2) HEDGE LAYER — baked into a second OffscreenCanvas, re-baked ONLY when a wall is added or destroyed. (L3) LIVE LAYER — pawns, footprints, ghosts, particles, HUD, drawn every frame. This keeps the per-frame cost to a few hundred ops.

GRAVEL CORRIDORS: fill the board rect with #CFC3A6. Bake a 256x256 noise tile once: draw ~9000 arcs of radius 0.5-1.6px at random positions, colours drawn from [#A8997A, #E4DAC0, #B5A585, #8F8064] at alpha 0.05-0.18. Then ctx.createPattern(tile,'repeat') and fill the corridor area. Add a very slow radial vignette on top: createRadialGradient(cx,cy,0.15*size,cx,cy,0.72*size) from rgba(0,0,0,0) to rgba(20,12,4,0.22).

HEDGE SLABS (the signature): each wall is one rounded-rect of length 2 cells x thickness 0.30 cell, radius 0.32*cell, built in five passes with a per-wall PRNG seeded by hash(c,r,orientation) so texture is stable across re-bakes. (1) Fake soft shadow WITHOUT ctx.filter: three stacked rounded-rects offset (1,2)/(2,3)/(3,5) at rgba(6,16,4) alpha .18/.14/.10. (2) Body: createLinearGradient across the slab's SHORT axis, stops #24460F @0 -> #2F5C1C @0.30 -> #5F9A26 @0.78 -> #4A7D20 @1, with the light vector fixed at (-0.5,-0.85) so every hedge on the board is lit identically. (3) Crown: inset rounded-rect at 62% of the short axis, gradient #6FAE2E -> #91CD53; then a second inset at 30% filled #A8CF62. (4) Foliage: 40-70 filled arcs of r 0.9-2.2px, colours from [#3E7A19, #6FAE2E, #91CD53, #B7DC72] at alpha .35-.70, jittered inside the slab and biased toward the crown, EACH preceded by a 1px darker arc at offset (+0.6,+0.8) in #1B3A10 so they read as leaf clumps with depth. (5) 2-3 berry dots in #E4B84A at alpha 0.5. Seeded and placed hedges are drawn identically (they are all hedge); a freshly placed one additionally gets a 1.5px owner-coloured root line along its base for two full rounds.

BOARD FRAME: a 14px band in #24406E, with a 2px #FFC100 inner keyline and a 1px #0E1C36 outer keyline, drawn as a stroked rounded-rect. At each of the eight compass points, an inward-pointing gold triangle (moveTo/lineTo/closePath), filled #FFC100 when unclaimed and with the seat's player colour when claimed — a direct lift of the amber shift arrows I saw on the Ravensburger board edge.

THE HEART: a circle r = 1.15*cell at board centre, filled with createRadialGradient (#F4EEDC @0 -> #E8DFC8 @0.6 -> #D9CDAF @1). Engrave the seven-circuit Cretan labyrinth over it as a Path2D built from an inline SVG 'd' string (arcs + lines, ~40 commands, hard-coded as text, scaled with ctx.scale to the disc), stroked in #B9A87E with lineWidth 0.09*r, lineCap 'round' and lineJoin 'round' — the rounded caps are what make it read as the reference. Over that, a 2px #FFC100 ring with alpha oscillating 0.25-0.70 at 0.6Hz.

PAWNS: (1) ground shadow — ellipse rx 0.34c ry 0.20c at (+0.10c,+0.16c) rgba(0,0,0,0.35), plus a larger ellipse rx 0.44c ry 0.26c at alpha 0.15. (2) body — circle r 0.30c with createRadialGradient centred at (-0.10c,-0.12c): stop 0 = colour lightened 45%, stop 0.55 = base colour, stop 1 = colour darkened 30%. (3) rim — 1.5px stroke of colour darkened 55%. (4) specular — ellipse rx 0.11c ry 0.07c at (-0.10c,-0.12c) in rgba(255,255,255,0.55) plus a 1px arc from 190deg to 300deg in rgba(255,255,255,0.35). (5) 3-letter tag in 10px Space Grotesk 700, colour auto-contrast (luminance>0.55 -> #1A1712 else #FFF), drawn inside a ctx.save/rotate matched to that player's seat. (6) Torch holder: extra ring r 0.42c stroked 2px #FFE28A with pulsing alpha, plus six 4px radial ticks.

FOOTPRINTS / GHOSTS / PARTICLES: footprint = rounded-square radius 0.25c, 2px stroke in player colour at 0.9 alpha, fill same colour at 0.14 alpha, plus a centre dot r 0.10c. Ghost hedge = the full slab render at globalAlpha 0.45 with a 2px dashed (setLineDash([5,4])) outline in the player colour; illegal = outline #C81E1E plus four diagonal hatch strokes. Particles are a flat array of {x,y,vx,vy,r,color,life} integrated at 380px/s^2 gravity — never more than 80 alive.

TEXT ROTATION: every seat-facing element is drawn inside ctx.save(); ctx.translate(px,py); ctx.rotate(seatAngle); ...; ctx.restore(), where seatAngle = (slot/8)*2PI + PI for the outward-facing convention. No DOM, no CSS transforms needed.

FONTS: load Cormorant Garamond 600 italic and Space Grotesk 500/700 from the approved list via a Google Fonts stylesheet link; give both a real fallback stack ('Space Grotesk', system-ui, sans-serif / 'Cormorant Garamond', Georgia, serif) and re-bake L1 once document.fonts.ready resolves so baked labels are not stuck in the fallback.

## Risks

- Tap targets: at 9x9 the wall gaps are only a few real pixels wide. Mitigated entirely by the RUN/HEDGE mode toggle (only one target class live at a time) plus 22px snap-to-nearest-legal-slot. Do NOT ship a mode-less board — the two target classes will fight and the game becomes unplayable.
- 8-player downtime: seven turns of waiting per player. Mitigated by 2-step runs at P>=5, a 15s clock with auto-run on expiry, single-tap moves, and a shorter target race length (8 steps at 8p vs 17 at 2p). If playtests still drag, cut wall budget to 2 at 7-8p.
- Wall-legality cost: rule (3) needs a BFS per pawn per candidate slot. Never run it live during a drag. Precompute the full legal-slot set ONCE per turn (64 slots x P x 81 cells ~ 50k ops) and look it up; re-run only after a wall commits or a Shears fires.
- Colour confusion at 8 players over a green-and-tan field. Mitigated by the fixed 8-ramp that deliberately contains no green (green belongs to hedges alone), by 3-letter tags rendered on every pawn, and by the seat plate colour swatches on the frame. Test crimson vs rose and azure vs violet specifically.
- Baked-layer memory: the hedge layer at 3x DPR on a 360px board is ~1080^2 ~ 4.6MB. Cap the bake at 2x DPR, re-bake only on wall change, and reuse a single OffscreenCanvas rather than allocating a new one per bake.
- The fairness regeneration loop could in principle spin. Cap at 200 attempts, then fall back to accepting 'all start distances within 1 of each other'; if even that fails, fall back to an empty (Quoridor-pure) board, which is trivially symmetric. Also note this game is a synthesis of Quoridor + Ravensburger Labyrinth, not a port of an existing title, so the numbers (budgets, step counts, target distances) are starting points that need one real playtest pass at 2p and at 8p.
