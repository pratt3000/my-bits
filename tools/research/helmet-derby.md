# helmet-derby — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

Chunky low-resolution pixel art (roughly 200x150 logical pixels upscaled 4-5x with nearest-neighbour), where EVERY sprite carries a hard 1px pure-black #000000 keyline — that outline is the single most identity-defining trait and nothing in the frame is drawn without it. The world is a dark, grimy gladiator stadium: a near-black plum void (#0E0815) at the edges, a speckled maroon-purple crowd wall (#492B3C base with #5C3A4A/#6B4552/#7E5666 noise dots) lit by warm spotlight cones, unreadable sponsor banners in dark #39232E with #C8A05A block-lettering, and giant grey sawblades (#474450) on shafts flanking the arena. Against that murk sits the arena floor as a single wavy heightfield rendered as a hot vertical gradient — a 1-2px cream crust (#F9E791/#F7E28E) that reads as a glowing rim light, falling through #EAC26D, #D39E50, #CB8A41 to a burnt #A45F2D at depth, dithered at the band boundaries. The two cars are the only saturated hue in the frame: pure cobalt blue (#1E5FD0 body, #0943B6 wheel hubs, #55C2FF specular) versus fire-engine red (#D3383C body, #B90F10 shadow, #FD8B85 specular), each carrying a tiny helmeted driver whose head is a 5x5 pixel blob with a near-black visor. Impacts detonate as pure-white cores blowing out through electric yellow #FFFF42, gold #FFD532, orange #FF7915 to a deep red #B40002 rim, with white 1px spark rays and tumbling debris pixels — the KO is the whole point of the composition and gets the brightest values on screen. Everything is grid-snapped: positions round to integer pixel units and sprite rotation quantizes to 32 steps so cars visibly click around rather than smoothly spinning.

## Palette

- `#000000` — Mandatory 1px hard keyline outlining every sprite, glyph and UI shape — the core identity trait
- `#0E0815` — Deep void background: sky above the stadium, cave interior, letterbox bars
- `#492B3C` — Stadium crowd wall base plum, over-speckled with 6 lighter noise dots
- `#F9E791` — Terrain crust highlight — the 1-2px glowing cream rim along the top of every arena surface
- `#D39E50` — Terrain mid-band sand/rock
- `#A45F2D` — Terrain deep burnt-orange base fill
- `#1E5FD0` — BLUE team primary: car body, button face top, name-tag tint
- `#55C2FF` — BLUE highlight: helmet glint, button top-edge bevel, filled score star (#188FF1 variant)
- `#D3383C` — RED team primary: car body, button face top, name-tag tint
- `#FD8B85` — RED highlight: helmet glint, button top-edge bevel; red filled star is the darker #BF141B
- `#FFFF42` — Impact/explosion electric yellow, second ring out from the white core
- `#FF7915` — Impact/explosion orange, fourth ring, fading to #B40002 at the rim

## Typography

Press Start 2P is the correct primary and it is not a compromise — the reference game's HUD, score pips and title lockups are a squarish bitmap face with flat terminals, uniform monospace-ish widths and a heavy black outline, which is exactly Press Start 2P's construction. Use it for: score/crown counts, the round timer, "SUDDEN DEATH", "SELF KO!", "GO!", button labels, player names (truncate to 7 glyphs), and the seat card headline. Critical rendering rule: do NOT use strokeText for the outline — at 8-12px it produces mushy joins. Instead draw the string 8 times in #000000 at ±1px offsets (N/NE/E/SE/S/SW/W/NW), then once in #FDFEFF on top, with all fillText coordinates snapped to the integer pixel grid times the scale factor. Bebas Neue is the mandatory secondary for any string Press Start 2P would overflow on a 390px-wide portrait screen — arena names, seat-card instructions ("BOTH PLAYERS SIT ON THE SAME EDGE"), format descriptions, settings copy. Bebas Neue's tall condensed all-caps reads as stadium/derby signage and sits comfortably next to the pixel face without fighting it. Do not use any of the other six approved fonts; a serif or a soft humanist sans would instantly destroy the arcade identity.

## Shape language

Axis-aligned rectangles first, circles second, nothing else. Cars are assembled from 6-14 stacked rects in local space (chassis slab, cab block, ram/blade, roll-cage bars as three thin rects, spoiler) plus 1-3 wheels; there are almost no diagonals except deliberate 45-degree pixel staircases on dragster noses and plough blades. Wheels are true pixel circles — generated by a midpoint-circle row scan and filled as one 1px-tall rect per row, never ctx.arc — with three concentric bands: black #000000 tyre, #2A2E2C inner tread, team-colour hub (#0943B6 blue / #DF0000 red) with 4 spoke pixels that rotate with wheel spin. The driver is a 3x4 torso rect, two 1x3 arm rects, a 1x2 neck, and a 5x5 head built from row widths [3,5,5,5,3] so it reads as a rounded helmet. Terrain is the opposite language: one long organic sine-sum heightfield with no straight edges at all, which is what makes the rigid rectangular cars pop against it. UI shapes are rounded rects with a fat 8px corner radius rendered as a stack of 1px rows following a quarter-circle — soft-cornered chunky slabs, never sharp panels. Stars are 10-point pixel stars from a hardcoded 11x11 boolean string bitmap. The overall rule: hard silhouettes, no anti-aliasing anywhere, no thin lines below 1 logical pixel.

## UI chrome

Sampled directly from the live in-game screenshot. SCORE: five pixel stars per player pinned to the top corners — blue's fill left-to-right from the screen's left edge, red's fill right-to-left from the right edge, so both players' progress grows toward the centre. Filled blue #188FF1, filled red #BF141B, empty #4B4454, all with a 1px #000000 outline and a 1px #FFFFFF upper-left glint. BUTTONS: chunky rounded-rect slabs with a 3-zone vertical construction — (a) a bright top-edge highlight 2px tall (#55C2FF blue / #FD8B85 red), (b) a face with a vertical gradient (blue #1E5FD0 at top to #2E8FF6 at the bottom of the face; red #D3383C to #F35D5C), (c) a hard band boundary then a darker "skirt" that gives the 3D extrusion (blue #1E348C to #1E46AA, base #202977; red #881B23 to #AA252C, base #74151E), plus a single 1px inner bounce-line near the bottom (#4DB9FF / #FE837D). Glyphs are two-tone bevelled chevrons: white #FDFEFF on the upper half, pale blue #B4D4FF on the lower half — note the glyph shading is the SAME pale blue on the red buttons, it is not tinted. Pressed state translates the whole slab down 3 logical px and kills the top highlight. PAUSE: a smaller slate slab, face #323A54 to #454C74, highlight #6473A7, same white/#B4D4FF two-bar glyph. NAME TAGS (multiplayer): a dark semi-transparent rounded plaque with a 1px black outline, text in bright cyan #74EBFF for the blue side and salmon #FFACA4 for the red side, floating above each car with a small downward tail. There is no chrome around the arena viewport itself — the world bleeds to the screen edges and the control deck sits directly on top of it.

## Motion

Heavy, over-tuned, comedic. Cars are high-mass and lightly damped so they flip constantly and land badly; the driver's head is on a soft spring joint and visibly flops with an offset of sin(t)*chassisAngularVelocity, which sells every crash. Movement is grid-snapped — positions round to integers before drawing so slow motion is visibly steppy, and sprite rotation quantizes to 32 discrete steps (11.25 degrees) so cars click around like rotating pixel art rather than smoothly-transformed bitmaps. The KO is a scripted celebration, not an instant: (1) 90-140ms full hitstop freeze-frame, (2) screen shake starting at 8px amplitude decaying over 380ms via translate offsets, (3) the explosion blooms out over ~450ms, (4) camera punch-zooms toward the victim x1.35 over 200ms on easeOutBack, (5) ~1.1s of slow motion at timeScale 0.35 while debris tumbles, (6) the new score star pops in scaling 0 to 1.45 to 1.0 over 320ms (easeOutBack) with a white radial flash, (7) reset. Camera continuously fits both cars with 20% padding, lerping at 0.08 per frame, clamped to arena bounds and to a minimum zoom so cars never shrink below ~14px tall. Idle life: cars bob 1px on their suspension, ~2% of crowd speckle pixels swap brightness per frame to read as a moving audience, banner lights strobe every 900ms. Sudden death: a red vignette of two concentric rgba(190,0,0,a) edge bands pulses at 1.6Hz with alpha oscillating 0.10-0.28, and each countdown numeral scale-punches to 1.5 then settles. Buttons depress 3px in 40ms and spring back with a 1px overshoot. Everything eases OUT hard and fast; nothing eases in slowly.

## Mechanics

OBJECTIVE: hit the opponent's HEAD. The head is a small separate collider on a spring joint above the driver's torso; the car body is not a target. CONTROLS: exactly two buttons per player. FWD (right chevron) applies wheel motor torque in +x on the ground and nose-down chassis torque in the air; BACK (left chevron) applies -x wheel torque on the ground and nose-up chassis torque in the air. Both held = brake, no net air torque. There is no jump and no flip button — a capsized car rights itself by pumping BACK/FWD, which is the skill floor. Some vehicles have PROPULSION: FWD additionally applies a thrust impulse along the facing vector, including in mid-air. SCORING: exactly one point per round to exactly one player, awarded the instant a head collider is killed. Kill conditions, all of which score for the OPPONENT of the dead driver: (a) opponent's body/wheel/attached weapon overlaps your head with relative speed >= the KO threshold (~3.2 world-units/s); (b) your head contacts static terrain at impact speed >= threshold — SELF-KO STILL AWARDS THE POINT TO THE OTHER PLAYER, this is the defining comedy rule and must be labelled "SELF KO!" on screen so it reads as intentional; (c) your head enters lethal liquid (water/lava/acid) — instant, no speed threshold; (d) your head is hit by a hazard (sawblade, meteor, mine blast, flaming cow); (e) your car leaves the arena bounds sideways or below the kill plane. Head contact BELOW the speed threshold does NOT kill — you can rest your head on the ground gently, which is what makes tipping over survivable. DOUBLE KO: if both heads die within the same 100ms window both players score; if that would take both to the target, drop both to target-1 and play a single decider round. ROUND STRUCTURE: cars spawn upright and stationary at 25% and 75% of arena width, input locked through a 3-2-1-GO countdown (~1.6s), then live. SUDDEN DEATH: a round timer runs from 0; at t=18s with no KO a 5-second countdown appears, at t=23s the arena hazard begins and never stops until someone dies (these exact seconds are my calibration — the source only says "a short period of time" then a 5-second countdown). Hazard per arena: SAWBLADES (3-5 blades descend from the ceiling at ~6 units/s and halt at 40% arena height, leaving survivable gaps directly between them — roofed arenas only); RISING FLOOD (liquid plane rises at ~5 units/s, only in arenas with a basin; each KO drops the level and it refills); METEORS (spawn rate ramping 0.8 to 4 per second from random x, open-top arenas only, extinguished by water); MINES (like meteors but detonate on terrain contact with a 22-unit knockback radius — unavoidable, only pushable); FLAMING COWS (larger collider, never stop spawning, open-top only). VEHICLE VARIETY is a real mechanic, not cosmetic: roofed vehicles (bubble car, bulldozer, fire truck) shield the head from falling hazards; tall vehicles (monster truck) put the head close to descending sawblades; bikes expose the driver almost completely; low dragsters are hard to hit but flip easily. POWERUPS (optional toggle): a bobbing crate spawns every 8-12s at a reachable point, attaches on contact until KO or timeout — SAWBLADE (blade on a 14-unit chain swinging counter-clockwise, passes through all geometry to hit heads directly), MISSILES (inaccurate rocket every 1.4s, knockback on explode), ZAPPER (charges 4s then fires one unstoppable bolt at the nearest head), SUPER RAM (x2.2 knockback with a white ripple ring), SPEED (x1.5 wheel torque), MEDUSA (non-damaging shove pulse every 2.5s), TYREHOOK (destroys one enemy tyre per latch, no effect on wheel-less vehicles). MATCH: default first-to-3 head-hits wins a bout (the source game uses 5; 3 keeps hot-seat turns short).

## One-phone adaptation

HIDDEN INFORMATION: there is none. This is a pure-skill real-time physics duel, so NO privacy screen is required for the core loop — do not invent one. The only optional hidden-info flow is BLIND PICK (below). SEATING — the hard problem: this is a side-view driving game, so a player reading the screen upside-down would see cars driving on the ceiling. Opposite-side seating therefore CANNOT share one upright viewport. Two solutions, both shipped: (1) DUO, the default. Both live players sit shoulder-to-shoulder on the BOTTOM long edge, phone portrait and flat. One big upright arena. On a 400x880 virtual canvas: y0-64 match HUD (blue name + 3 star pips left, red name + pips right, timer centred); y64-560 arena viewport (400x496, camera shows ~180 world units wide); y560-600 arena-name / hazard ticker; y600-880 control deck. Blue cluster x10-186 (BACK x10-94, FWD x102-186), red cluster mirrored x214-390, each button 84 wide x 150 tall at y700-850, with each player's own name+pip card at y610-690 above their own buttons so they never look away. A hard 28px dead gutter at x186-214 carries a hazard-stripe divider so no thumb can cross. Pause is a 44x44 pad at x178-222, y836-880, tucked in the gutter below both clusters. (2) FACE-OFF, a setup toggle for players across a table. Split the screen at y=440 and render the SAME physics world twice: the bottom half upright (HUD y440-500, arena y500-730, controls y730-880 as one centred cluster, BACK x60-190 and FWD x210-340, 130px tall), the top half identical but drawn inside ctx.save(); translate(200,220); rotate(PI); ... restore(). Both players get an upright arena and upright chevrons. Because left/right in the world is mirrored for the rotated player, float a bobbing colour-matched "▼ YOU" caret above each player's own car in that player's own viewport. 3-8 PLAYERS: two live at a time, hot-seat rotation — this matches the source game's own King of the Hill structure and is the only honest answer for one phone. Setup screen: +/- to set count 2-8, tap a slot to name (defaults P1-P8), pick from an 8-swatch pixel palette (#1E5FD0 blue, #D62218 red, #3FA83A green, #F2C230 yellow, #A24BD6 purple, #31C7C7 cyan, #F07C1E orange, #F268A8 pink); the two live players always render in their own colours and the whole HUD/button chrome retints to match. Format toggle: LADDER (winner stays as King, next in queue takes the loser's seat, first to K crowns wins, K defaults to the player count clamped 3-9) or GAUNTLET (full round-robin, every pair once, most crowns). BETWEEN BOUTS: a full-screen SEAT CARD held for a 2.5s minimum AND gated on two separate READY pads — one placed inside each incoming player's control zone — so the phone physically cannot advance until both people have taken their seats. The card shows huge colour blocks, "◀ <NAME> SITS LEFT" / "<NAME> SITS RIGHT ▶" in Bebas Neue, and the crown standings as a column of pixel crowns. OPTIONAL BLIND PICK (the only privacy flow): after the seat card, each live player in turn gets an opaque full-screen card in their own colour reading "PASS TO <NAME> — HOLD TO REVEAL", requiring a 600ms press-and-hold on a pad positioned at that player's own edge; then a 3-vehicle pick grid; then the card goes opaque again before the phone moves on, so neither player sees the other's counter-pick.

## Drawing it with no assets

RENDERING CORE: work entirely in low-resolution integer "pixel units" and never draw a sub-pixel shape. Define an integer scale S (4 or 5) and a single helper px(x,y,w,h,col) that does ctx.fillStyle=col; ctx.fillRect(Math.round(x)*S, Math.round(y)*S, w*S, h*S). Every visual is built from px() plus a pixel-circle row scan. Set ctx.imageSmoothingEnabled=false globally. This needs only one canvas and never touches document.createElement. Optionally cache the static arena background in `new OffscreenCanvas(w,h)` (constructor, not createElement) guarded by typeof OffscreenCanvas !== 'undefined', with a full re-draw fallback. Do NOT use ctx.filter (banned) — all glow and blur is faked with concentric hard-edged bands of decreasing alpha. TERRAIN: a heightfield array h[i] for i in 0..worldWidth, built as the sum of 3 sine waves with per-bout seeded random phase/amplitude plus a seeded value-noise ridge; clamp and smooth once. Draw as 1px-wide vertical columns from h[i] to the bottom, colouring each pixel by depth-below-crust through a LUT: depth 0-1 #F9E791, 2-4 #EAC26D, 5-9 #D39E50, 10-18 #CB8A41, 19-30 #BE7635, 31+ #A45F2D. Dither the band boundaries by seeded-randomly pushing the transition +/-1px per column. Speckle 3% of mid-band pixels 12% darker for grit. Put a 1px #FFF6D0 rim on the top pixel of upward-facing columns. Physics collides directly against h[i] sampled at each wheel's x — no polygon solver needed. CROWD/STADIUM: base fill #492B3C, then 3 createRadialGradient spotlight cones from rgba(255,235,200,0.16) to transparent, then a seeded loop plotting ~1400 single pixels from ['#5C3A4A','#6B4552','#7E5666','#3A232D','#8B6A78','#412F3D'] with vertical jitter (re-roll 2% per frame for the crowd shimmer), then 4-6 sponsor banners as #39232E rects with a 1px #1A1017 outline and 3-4px #C8A05A blocks faking text. CARS: a parts grammar, not sprites. Each vehicle is data: {body:[[x,y,w,h,role],...], wheels:[[wx,wy,r]], seat:[sx,sy], roof:bool, ram:[x,y,w,h]}. Roles resolve to PRIMARY (team hue), DARK (team hue x0.45), LIGHT (team hue lerped 0.55 to white), TRIM #2A2E2C, GLASS #E0FBFE. Draw each rect twice — first 1px larger in #000000, then the fill — to get the mandatory keyline. Ship 8 archetypes: Monster Truck (tall slab, wheels r=7), Dragster (long low slab, front r=3 + rear r=6, 45-degree staircase nose), Bike (one r=8 wheel, tiny body, driver fully exposed), Bulldozer (blade rect at the front, low roof over the seat), Tank (turret rect plus tracks as a row of 6 small rects), Buggy (roll cage = 3 thin arcing rects), Bubble Car (semicircular head shield), Fire Truck (long body with a roof). Transform with ctx.translate(x,y) then ctx.rotate(quantize(angle,32)). WHEELS: midpointCircleRows(r) returning half-widths per row, filled as one 1px-tall rect per row — black #000000 tyre, #2A2E2C tread, team hub (#0943B6 / #DF0000), 4 spoke pixels rotated by wheel spin. DRIVER: 3x4 torso rect, two 1x3 arms, 1x2 neck, 5x5 head from row widths [3,5,5,5,3] in #1571ED or #D62218, with a lower-right 2-row shadow, a 2px #7ADBFF upper-left specular, and a 3x2 near-black #101018 visor. EXPLOSIONS: 5-9 overlapping pixel-circles of decreasing radius over life t, radii r0*(0.3+t), coloured outward #FFFFFF, #FFFF42, #FFD532, #FF7915, #B40002; plus 6-10 white 1px spark rays plotted along Bresenham lines of length 8+16t at random angles; plus 8-14 ballistic tumbling debris pixels. STARS/CROWNS: an 11x11 boolean bitmap encoded as a '.#' string literal (data, not an asset), or an inline Path2D star path filled at low-res so it quantizes; fill #188FF1 / #BF141B, empty #4B4454, 1px #000000 outline, 1px #FFFFFF glint. BUTTONS: roundRectRows(w,h,radius=8) returning per-row insets from a quarter-circle, filled row by row from a vertical gradient LUT with the 3-zone highlight/face/skirt construction and a hard band boundary; chevron glyph as a Path2D filled #FDFEFF then overdrawn on its lower rows in #B4D4FF. HAZARDS: sawblade = pixel-circle #474450 + 12 Path2D triangle teeth in #8E8A9C + #2A2833 hub, spinning 6 rad/s on a #3A3745 shaft rect; flood = a solid rgba(30,120,200,0.72) rect whose top edge is a 1px #B4E8FF line displaced by sin(x*0.15 + t*3)*1.5; meteor = #FF7915 circle with a #FFFF42 core and a 5-pixel flame trail; mine = a #2A2833 circle with 6 #8E8A9C spike rects; flaming cow = a 9x6 white blob with 3 black patch rects, 4 leg rects and a flame on top. FONTS: Google Fonts link for Press Start 2P and Bebas Neue only, with a fallback stack of monospace and sans-serif; snap all fillText coords to the S grid and use the 8-offset black-shadow outline technique. Total code well under 2MB with zero packaged or remote assets.

## Risks

- Physics tuning IS the game — a naive general rigid-body solver will feel floaty or explode. Use a purpose-built model: chassis capsule + 1-3 wheel circles joined by spring-dampers, fixed 120Hz timestep with 4 sub-steps, gravity ~52 units/s^2, restitution ~0.25, friction ~0.9, and collide wheels straight against the heightfield array rather than polygons.
- A portrait arena is narrow: naive fit-both-cars camera will zoom out until cars are 10px tall and the 5px head target is unhittable and invisible. Clamp minimum zoom so cars never render below ~14px tall, and keep the world only ~200 units wide so the fit rarely binds.
- Four buttons across a ~390px width with two adult hands means mis-presses and cross-talk. Mandatory: the 28px dead gutter, hit rectangles extended out to the physical screen edges (bigger than the drawn slab), and per-touch-identifier tracking so a finger that starts on a button stays held even if it drifts off.
- Multi-touch on iOS Safari will drop held buttons — touchcancel fires on system gestures and rubber-band scroll. Must preventDefault on touchstart/touchmove, set touch-action:none, and on every touchend/touchcancel re-derive the full held-button set from e.touches rather than incrementally clearing state.
- getBoundingClientRect is banned, so touch-to-canvas mapping must come from somewhere else: pin the canvas position:fixed at 0,0 filling the visual viewport and map touch.clientX/Y directly by the canvas.width/window.innerWidth ratio. Test with the iOS URL bar both expanded and collapsed.
- Press Start 2P is extremely wide — a 7-character name at 10px already spans ~70px, so long names will overflow HUD slots and seat cards. Truncate hard to 7 glyphs in the pixel face and switch to Bebas Neue for any longer string; and surface a clear 'SELF KO!' callout, because self-KO awarding the point to the opponent reads as a bug to newcomers unless it is loudly labelled.
