# headsup — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

Loud, flat, high-saturation party-app design with zero gradients on flat UI and exactly one signature texture. What I saw in the App Store screenshots and icon: the whole app sits on a single electric cobalt blue field (#0C7DFD sampled) with white ultra-bold sans headlines stacked 3-4 lines deep, and lighter sky-blue (#20BAFE) rounded speech bubbles with triangular tails floating as clue callouts. The hero object is the word card: a very rounded rectangle (corner radius ~9% of card width) built as three concentric shapes — a soft dark drop shadow, then a thick pure-white border ring ~6% of card width, then a deeper blue interior (#1364D7 to #156EEC, subtly lighter at the top-left) filled edge-to-edge with a fine diagonal diamond lattice of thin darker lines, like a pie-crust or argyle grid. The word ("Rollercoaster") sits in huge white heavy sans, tightly tracked, with the timer ("0:47") in the same face at ~45% size directly below. The CORRECT and PASS full-screen states are the same card motif recoloured and reframed: a near-black #212121 bezel, a white inner ring inset inside it, and a textured fill of pure green #03C902 (on a mint #17CF83 full-bleed background) or orange #FF8A00 (on a yellow #FFD02A background), with a fat white arrow curving down or up beside it. Nothing is subtle, nothing is grey, and every surface is either a flat brand colour or that lattice.

- `#20BAFE` — Sky blue — speech bubbles, secondary chips, clue callouts (sampled)
- `#14C6FF` — Bright cyan — section bands, headers, deck-browser banner (sampled)

- `#212121` — Near-black bezel frame and outline layer around CORRECT/PASS cards (sampled)
- `#03C902` — CORRECT green — textured fill of the correct card and the GOT IT button (sampled)
- `#17CF83` — Mint green — full-bleed background of the CORRECT flash screen (sampled)
- `#FF8A00` — PASS orange — textured fill of the pass card and the Pass button (sampled)
- `#FFD02A` — Pass yellow — full-bleed background of the PASS flash screen (sampled)
- `#F8C307` — Deck gold — secondary panels, deck-select surfaces, score plaques (sampled)

## Typography

Use **Nunito Sans** as the primary and near-exclusive face. The "Heads Up!" wordmark in the icon and the in-card word are a heavy humanist sans with a very large x-height, wide round bowls, open apertures and noticeably soft, slightly rounded stroke terminals — friendly and shouty rather than corporate. Of the eight approved fonts Nunito Sans is by far the closest: it has the same humanist skeleton and soft terminals and ships weights up to 900/1000. Assign: Nunito Sans 900 for card words, CORRECT/PASS labels and button text (letter-spacing -0.02em, all-caps only for the two state words, sentence case for the guess word exactly as the reference does); Nunito Sans 800 for screen headlines (set 3-4 short lines, line-height 1.05, centred, like the marketing screens); Nunito Sans 700 for deck names, player names and body copy. Use **Bebas Neue** for exactly two things — the giant 3-2-1 handoff countdown numerals and the final score numerals — where a single condensed glyph reads as a poster and cannot be confused. Do not use Inter (too neutral, apertures too closed for the friendly feel), Space Grotesk (technical quirk fights the party tone), or Press Start 2P (wrong era entirely). Never set the guess word in Bebas Neue: a long word like "Rollercoaster" needs Nunito Sans's width and round bowls to be readable across a table.

## Shape language

Everything is a rounded rectangle or a circle; there are no sharp corners anywhere except the deliberate triangular speech-bubble tails and the arrow heads. Corner radius is generous and proportional, not fixed: word card r = 0.09 x cardW, buttons and chips r = 0.5 x height (full pill on small controls, ~0.28 x height on wide buttons as seen on the "You got it!" / "Pass!" bar), the app-icon card r ≈ 0.20 x width. The dominant compositional device is CONCENTRIC FRAMING — every important object is 3 or 4 nested rounded rects with different fills (shadow, black bezel, white ring, textured colour), each inset by a constant proportion, which reads as a phone-in-a-frame and is the single most identifiable structural signature after the colour. Buttons carry a small downward-pointing triangular tail (~22px wide, 16px deep) making them read as speech bubbles rather than chrome. Arrows are fat, hand-drawn-feeling curved strokes with a solid triangular head and 2-3 short motion ticks trailing the tail. Type is set in tight centred blocks that behave as shapes themselves. Absolutely no thin lines, no hairline dividers, no drop-shadow-as-depth-hierarchy — only one heavy shadow under the hero card.

## UI chrome

Chrome is minimal because the colour does the work. Buttons: wide rounded rects filled flat (#03C902 green, #FF8A00 orange), white 900-weight label, a downward triangular tail centred on the bottom edge, and a hard 4px darker bottom edge (a "chunky candy button" ledge, not a blur) so they look pressable. Full-screen state cards: black #212121 bezel rounded rect + white ring inset ~8/415 of screen width + textured colour fill inset a further ~8, with one soft dark shadow offset down-right. Toasts: white rounded pill with a tail and coloured 900-weight text (I saw a white "You got it!" pill with green text floating above the card). No status bar, no nav bar, no tab bar visible during play — the card owns the screen. Panels in the deck browser are full-bleed horizontal colour bands (gold #F8C307, cyan #14C6FF, brown) each with a white 800-weight italic-feeling headline and a horizontally scrolling row of portrait deck tiles with white borders. Borders are always white and always thick; there is never a 1px stroke.

## Motion

Cartoon-snappy and over-committed — every state change is loud and fast, nothing fades politely. New word card: slides up from below the fold over 220ms with easeOutBack (overshoot ~8%), word text scales 0.94 to 1.0 over 160ms behind it. CORRECT: full-screen hard cut to #17CF83 for 90ms, then the green bezel card pops in with a 1.0 to 1.12 to 1.0 scale on easeOutBack over 260ms, holds ~420ms total, plus a burst of 40-60 confetti rects with random spin and gravity. PASS: full-screen cut to #FFD02A, orange card enters from below and the outgoing word card slides UP and off (matching the physical tilt-up gesture) over 240ms easeOutCubic. Timer: digits sit still above 10s, then pulse scale 1.0 to 1.15 over 120ms on each tick under 10s, colour white to #F8C307 at 15s to #E8342A at 5s. Foul/invalid tap: 300ms decaying horizontal shake, amplitude 10px, sin(t*40)*e^(-6t). Handoff countdown: Bebas numerals scale 1.6 to 1.0 with easeOutBack and fade, one per second, with a rising WebAudio beep (440/554/659Hz, 80ms) and a long 880Hz tone on GO. Idle card: a 3px, 2.4s sine bob so the screen is never dead. Never use linear easing; never animate longer than 300ms except the deliberate 420ms state hold.

## Mechanics

CORE LOOP (exact): One player is the GUESSER and holds the device screen-outward against their forehead so everyone else can read it but they cannot. All other players are CLUE-GIVERS and describe the displayed word simultaneously, shouting. The guesser says guesses aloud. A round is a fixed timer, default 60s (30/60/90 selectable). Words are drawn one at a time from a shuffled deck. TILT-DOWN (device pitched face-down) = CORRECT: award +1 and immediately advance to the next word, showing a green CORRECT flash card. TILT-UP (device raised face-up above the head) = PASS: no score, no penalty, advance to the next word, showing an orange PASS flash card. The app auto-advances on gesture; there is no confirm step. When the timer hits zero the round ends immediately, mid-word — the word on screen at expiry is neither correct nor passed, it is discarded. CLUE RESTRICTIONS: clue-givers may not say the word or any part/compound of it, may not say "rhymes with", and may not say "starts with" or give a letter; everything else is legal (description, acting, sound effects, impressions, singing). Deck-specific overrides exist in the real app (Act It Out = gestures only and no speaking at all; Accents & Impressions = must perform, not describe). SCORING: score = number of correct words in the round; passed words are worth zero and carry no penalty. DECK HANDLING: draw without replacement from a shuffled deck; passed words go to a discard pile and do NOT reappear in the same round; if the deck exhausts mid-round, reshuffle the passed pile and continue. END OF ROUND: a review screen lists every word from the round tagged correct (green) or passed (orange), and — critically — each entry is TOGGLEABLE, so the group can correct a mis-registered tilt or strike an illegal clue after the fact; the score recomputes live from the toggles. The real app also records video of the clue-givers during the round for the review screen (not reproducible here; drop it). MATCH STRUCTURE: play proceeds until every player has guessed once (a "lap"); a match is a set number of laps (default 2). Individual mode: highest personal total wins. Team mode: split into two teams, alternate which team guesses each round, teammates give clues, totals are per-team, most correct after the agreed number of rounds wins. EDGE CASES TO GET RIGHT: (1) a gesture registered during the CORRECT/PASS flash card must be swallowed, not queued, or one tilt scores twice; (2) the timer keeps running during the flash card (this is authentic — the real app is criticised for it) so flashes must be short; (3) a guesser who says the exact word before the clue-givers finish still counts — correctness is called by the group, the device only records it; (4) with 2 players there is no team mode, only alternating individual rounds; (5) a round can end with zero correct, which is a legal score of 0.

## One-phone adaptation

This game is already single-device by nature — the adaptation problem is replacing tilt (unreliable/permission-gated on mobile web) and handling the INVERTED hidden information: the crowd may see the screen, the guesser must not. FOUR SCREEN MODES with different orientations: (1) LOBBY / SEATING — phone lies FLAT on the table, 2-8 players ringed around it. Draw a name plaque per player around the perimeter, each rotated to face its own edge (bottom edge 0°, top edge 180°, left 90°, right -90°); fill order bottom, top, bottom-left, top-right, ... so small groups never read upside-down. Each plaque is >=96x60 CSS px with a 14px gutter, tap to join/rename/assign team. A single circular START button of radius 0.20 x min(w,h) sits dead centre, orientation-neutral (ring + play triangle, no text), reachable from every seat and overlapping no plaque by construction. (2) HANDOFF / PRIVACY — the reverse privacy screen and the most important addition. Full-bleed cobalt, huge white text "PASS TO ALEX", "SCREEN AGAINST YOUR FOREHEAD — DON'T LOOK", an animated rotate-the-phone-sideways glyph, and one wide READY button. Tapping READY starts a 3-2-1 Bebas countdown with rising beeps; the first word is NOT rendered until the countdown completes, guaranteeing the guesser has time to raise the device before any word exists on screen. (3) PLAY — content rotated 90° so the phone is held in LANDSCAPE at the forehead and the crowd reads the word along the long axis at maximum type size. Input replaces tilt with two full-height EDGE BARS: left 22% of the landscape width = GOT IT (#03C902), right 22% = PASS (#FF8A00), each labelled vertically, leaving the central 56% for the word and DEAD to touch so a stray hand over the word cannot score. This works two ways at once — the guesser's own thumbs naturally rest on those two bars and can be found by feel without seeing, and any clue-giver can reach over and tap the coloured edge. If DeviceOrientationEvent is granted, tilt is layered on as a bonus, never as a requirement. A small FOUL chip lives on the TOP edge only (opposite the thumbs), requires a 400ms hold to fire, and skips the word with -1 — non-overlapping and deliberate. Every scored word shows a 1.2s UNDO chip in the bottom-left corner. (4) REVIEW / SCORE — phone back flat on the table, and the guesser may now look. Two-sided layout: in team mode split at the horizontal midline with the top half rotated 180° for the opposing team and the bottom half upright; in individual mode render upright with a persistent FLIP button that rotates the whole list 180° for someone sitting opposite. The word list entries are large toggle rows (>=64px tall) that swap between green/orange on tap, exactly as described in the mechanics.

## Drawing it with no assets

ZERO assets — everything is Canvas 2D paths. (1) THE LATTICE, the signature texture: clip to the object's roundRect, then stroke two families of parallel straight lines at +48° and -48° from horizontal, spacing = w/22, lineWidth = max(1, w/260), strokeStyle rgba(0,0,0,0.10). Using a low-alpha BLACK stroke over any fill automatically produces the correct "slightly darker lattice" on blue, green and orange with one code path. Bake one tile into an OffscreenCanvas (allowed — no document.createElement) and use ctx.createPattern for the full-screen backgrounds; stroke lines directly for small objects. (2) SOFT SHADOWS WITHOUT BLUR: since the filter/blur property is off the table, fake every shadow by stacking 6 concentric roundRects offset y +10, each expanded 3px more than the last, filled rgba(0,0,0,0.045) — the accumulating alpha reads as a soft penumbra and costs nothing. (3) THE HERO CARD: one helper draws N nested roundRects with proportional insets — shadow stack, then #212121 bezel, then #FFFFFF ring, then the textured colour fill — driven by a single {x,y,w,h,r,fill} spec so CORRECT/PASS/word cards are one function with different colours. (4) SPEECH BUBBLES: build the body roundRect and the triangular tail into ONE Path2D (moveTo/lineTo/arcTo around the corners, then out along the tail and back) so the fill has no seam. (5) THE EYES from the icon, for the title screen: each eye is two quadratic curves meeting at sharp canthi to form an almond; fill white; iris = circle with a createRadialGradient from #2E7FE0 at 30%/30% to #0A2E7A at the rim; pupil = solid #08122E circle at 0.45 iris radius; highlight = white circle r=0.22 iris radius at (-0.3r, -0.35r) plus a tiny second one; lower lid = a stroked arc in #E8A87C. (6) BACKGROUNDS: flat brand fill + the lattice pattern at 0.05 alpha + a createRadialGradient vignette (centre rgba(255,255,255,0.06) to edge rgba(0,0,0,0.10)). (7) TIMER: ctx.arc stroke, lineWidth 14, lineCap 'round', track rgba(0,0,0,0.18), sweep from -90° clockwise. (8) CONFETTI: 60 objects, each a filled rect drawn under a translate+rotate, colours sampled from the palette array, gravity 0.4, spin ±0.25 rad/frame. (9) ARROWS: a stroked quadratic curve with lineCap 'round' and lineWidth w/22, plus a separate filled triangle head, plus 3 short tick strokes at the tail. (10) TEXT FITTING: binary-search the font size with ctx.measureText so the word always fills 88% of the card width, wrapping to at most 2 lines, floor 40px. (11) ROTATED UI: every seat/panel renders inside ctx.save(); ctx.translate(cx,cy); ctx.rotate(angle); draw at origin; ctx.restore() — and hit-testing inverts the same transform manually (cos/sin on the touch point relative to the panel centre) since getBoundingClientRect is unavailable. Track canvas size from window.innerWidth/innerHeight and devicePixelRatio only.

## Risks

- Legibility across a table is the make-or-break: the word must be enormous (auto-fit to 88% card width, max 2 lines, 40px floor) or the game fails at 6-8 players in a room
- Mis-taps by clue-givers reaching over the device — mitigated by dead-zone centre, edge-only input bars, a 1.2s UNDO chip, and the toggleable end-of-round review; do not skip any of those three
- Tilt via DeviceOrientationEvent needs an explicit permission prompt on iOS and often silently fails — it must be a bonus layer only, never the required input path
- The landscape-content-in-a-portrait-canvas rotation confuses people on first launch; needs an explicit animated 'turn the phone sideways' hint on the handoff screen or players hold it wrong
- Word list must be embedded, family-safe and broad enough that decks don't repeat within a session — budget ~10 decks x 70 words; also avoid trademarked deck names (Harry Potter, Star Wars, Marvel) that the real app licenses
- Audio needs a WebAudio unlock on the first user gesture (the START button) or the countdown beeps and the final buzzer will be silent on iOS, which badly hurts the round-end feel
