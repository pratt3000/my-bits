# chess — design brief

*Compiled from reference images the researcher downloaded and viewed.*

## Art direction

I viewed four references: a straight top-down photo of a walnut-and-maple Staunton board (ref_chess_1), a low three-quarter view of the same set (ref_chess_2), a black-and-ivory plastic Staunton set on a light oak board shot at table level against black (ref_chess_3), and a macro row of golden sheesham Staunton pieces with a green-and-white checkered box behind them (ref_chess_5). The identity is warm, matte, tactile wood — NOT the flat vector look of a chess app. Light squares are a buttery maple cream that samples between #D4C196 in shadow and roughly #E9D9B4 in even light; dark squares are a mid walnut sampling #A6704E to #B5794F, with visible straight grain running in one direction per square and a faint per-square tonal drift so no two squares are identical. The playing field sits inside a wide (about 8% of board width) darker walnut frame sampling #875639 to #986F5B, carrying engraved cream coordinate glyphs A-H and 1-8 on all four sides, with the glyphs on the far two sides printed upside-down relative to the near ones — a detail worth stealing verbatim for a two-sided phone game. Pieces are honey boxwood (#C7A57B core, #E4CFA6 highlight) versus dark walnut (#4A3524 core, #866A57 rim-lit edge); in the plastic reference they are pure ivory #F0E8D8 against gloss near-black #12100E. Lighting in every reference is a single soft key from upper-left producing a short, tight contact shadow directly under each piece's flared base and a bright vertical highlight down the left flank of every turned column. The green board seen in ref_chess_5's box is a deep desaturated teal-green sampling #2D4148 to about #3E5F52 against off-white #C8CCC5 — that is the tournament-vinyl alternative, cooler and flatter than the wood.

## Typography

Primary display: Bebas Neue for player names, side labels ("WHITE TO MOVE"), the turn banner, and terminal states ("CHECKMATE", "STALEMATE"). It is tall, condensed, and all-caps, so it stays legible at 28-40px inside a 150px HUD band and — critically — reads just as well rotated 180 degrees for the opposite player, which humanist lowercase does not. Set it with 0.08em letterspacing in #E8D9B4 over dark wood plaques to mimic the engraved coordinate glyphs on the frame in ref_chess_1/2. Secondary UI: Inter 500/600 for menus, roster names, rules text, buttons, and the small a-h/1-8 board coordinates (Inter's tall x-height survives at 10px on a 45px square where a serif would mush). Tertiary: Space Mono 400 for the move list and clocks — SAN move pairs and mm:ss both need tabular alignment, and the typewriter texture reads as "score sheet", which is period-correct for a tournament board. Do NOT use Cormorant Garamond or DM Serif Display for anything small: they are tempting for the antique-wood mood but the hairline strokes disappear at mobile sizes and vanish entirely when rotated on a low-brightness phone laid flat on a table. If one serif accent is wanted, use DM Serif Display once, at 44px+, on the title/splash only.

## Shape language

Turned-on-a-lathe rotational symmetry. Every Staunton piece I looked at in ref_chess_5 is a stack of circular profiles: a broad flared base with a concave scotia sweeping up into a narrow waisted shaft, then a torus collar ring, then the identity finial. Pawn = plain sphere on a short collar. Bishop = mitre (an ogee-pointed dome) with a single angled slit and a small ball finial. Queen = coronet of five short spikes above a wide flared crown with a ball finial. King = tallest, crown band plus a bold Maltese-style cross finial. Rook = the only square-ish silhouette: a cylinder topped by four crenellations cut by three slots. Knight = the only asymmetric, hand-carved piece: an angled horse head with a deeply grooved mane of parallel notches, chiselled muzzle, drilled eye, and a chamfered rather than flared base. The rule: five of six pieces are pure convex curves and rings; the rook and knight break that on purpose, and that contrast is what makes a chess set readable at 45px. Board geometry is the opposite — hard orthogonal grid, zero corner radius on the 64 squares, one continuous seam line, and only the outer frame gets a soft 6-10px radius. Carry that duality into UI: board and grid are square and sharp, HUD chrome and buttons are turned and rounded (pill buttons, 12px radius plaques, circular avatar discs).

## UI chrome

The photographic references have no UI, so chrome must be extrapolated from the object itself — build every panel as a piece of the board's own furniture. HUD plaques: rounded-rect 12px radius filled with a vertical linear gradient #4A2F1C to #35210F, with a 2px inner bevel drawn as two strokes — rgba(214,168,110,0.35) along the top and left edges, rgba(0,0,0,0.5) along the bottom and right — exactly reproducing the frame bevel visible around the inner playing field in ref_chess_1. Text on plaques is engraved: draw the glyph once in rgba(0,0,0,0.55) offset +1px y, then again in #E8D9B4 on top. Primary buttons: 44px-tall pills, fill #2F2620, 1px border #8A6A45, Bebas Neue label letterspaced, with a 1px rgba(255,255,255,0.12) top inner line for the turned-brass read. Destructive (Resign) swaps border and label to #C4432E. Captured-material strip: a 22px-tall row of tiny 18px piece glyphs at 0.75 alpha plus a "+3" material delta in Space Mono. Board overlays follow app convention rather than photo convention: selected square = full-square rgba(242,193,74,0.50); last-move from and to = rgba(242,193,74,0.34); legal move onto an empty square = filled dot radius 0.16*sq in rgba(30,26,20,0.30); legal capture = ring, lineWidth 0.085*sq, radius 0.42*sq, same colour; king in check = six concentric circles stepping radius 0.16*sq to 0.56*sq with alpha ramping 0.55 to 0.0 in #C4432E (a hand-built radial falloff, since the blur/filter property is off-limits). Coordinate glyphs live in-square, lichess-style, at 22% square height in the opposite square's colour at 0.55 alpha: files on rank 1, ranks on file h. There is no modal dialog chrome except the promotion picker and the pass-the-phone privacy cover, both of which are full-bleed and drawn in the receiving player's rotation.

## Motion

Weighty and short — a real Staunton piece is bottom-heavy and lands with a click, so nothing should float or bounce. Pickup: on tap-select the piece scales 1.00 to 1.07 over 110ms with easeOutCubic while its contact shadow ellipse widens from rx 0.28*sq to 0.34*sq and drops alpha from 0.30 to 0.18 (the piece is "lifted"), plus navigator.vibrate(8). Travel: the moving piece is drawn on a top layer and interpolates along a straight line over 170ms easeOutCubic (knights arc slightly — add a 0.06*sq vertical lift at t=0.5 via a sine term, so the jump reads as a jump). Landing: a single 90ms squash to scaleY 0.96 then back, shadow snaps tight, vibrate(18). Capture: the taken piece scales to 0.62 and alpha to 0 over 140ms while eight 2-3px specks of its own body colour spray outward 0.3*sq with gravity — like chipped wood, not sparkles. Castling animates both pieces simultaneously with the rook offset 60ms later so the eye follows the king first. Promotion: the picker slides up 24px with a 200ms easeOutBack (overshoot 1.06), and the chosen piece morphs by cross-fading pawn out over 120ms while the new piece scales 0.7 to 1.0. Check: the king square's red rings pulse on a 900ms sine loop, alpha oscillating 0.35 to 0.65 — continuous, low-frequency, ignorable but present. Board flip (when auto-flip is on): rotate the whole board canvas 180 degrees over 420ms easeInOutCubic about its centre while the two HUD bands cross-fade their contents; pieces counter-rotate so they never appear upside down. Checkmate: overlay rgba(20,16,12,0.45) over 300ms, then topple the mated king — rotate it 26 degrees about its base point and translate it 0.18*sq along the fall direction over 380ms easeInQuad, ending with a 40ms settle. Idle: nothing moves except the check pulse and a 3s-period 4% alpha breathe on the active player's edge glow. Total motion budget: no animation over 450ms; the game must never make a player wait to tap.

## Mechanics

BOARD/SETUP. 8x8. a1 is a DARK square; each player has a light square at their bottom-right (h1 light for White). White rank 1: a1 R, b1 N, c1 B, d1 Q, e1 K, f1 B, g1 N, h1 R; pawns fill rank 2. Black mirrors on ranks 8 and 7. The queen starts on her own colour (white Q on light d1, black Q on dark d8). White moves first; players alternate; passing is not allowed.

MOVEMENT. King: one square in any of 8 directions, plus castling. Queen: any distance orthogonally or diagonally. Rook: any distance orthogonally. Bishop: any distance diagonally. Knight: (+/-1,+/-2) or (+/-2,+/-1), and is the only piece that jumps over occupied squares. Sliders stop at the first occupied square; they may capture an enemy there, never a friendly piece. Pawn: moves one square straight forward to an EMPTY square; from its own starting rank (2 for White, 7 for Black) it may instead advance two squares provided BOTH the intermediate and destination squares are empty; it captures only diagonally forward one square onto an enemy-occupied square; it may never move or capture backwards or straight forward onto an occupant.

LEGALITY. Generate pseudo-legal moves, make, test "is my own king attacked", unmake. A move is illegal if the mover's own king ends up attacked. This single test correctly handles pins, discovered checks, moving out of check, and the en-passant discovery edge case below. Never special-case pins.

CASTLING (all five conditions must hold). (1) The king and the chosen rook are both on their original squares and NEITHER has moved at any earlier point in the game. (2) Every square strictly between them is empty — f1,g1 for kingside; b1,c1,d1 for queenside. (3) The king is NOT currently in check. (4) The king neither passes through nor lands on a square attacked by any enemy piece — f1 and g1 for O-O, d1 and c1 for O-O-O. NOTE: b1 (and b8) may be attacked and castling is still legal; b1 need only be EMPTY. The rook may be attacked and may pass through attacked squares. (5) Execution: White O-O is Ke1->g1 with Rh1->f1; White O-O-O is Ke1->c1 with Ra1->d1; Black O-O is Ke8->g8 with Rh8->f8; Black O-O-O is Ke8->c8 with Ra8->d8. Rights: moving the king kills BOTH of that side's castling rights permanently; moving a rook (or that rook being captured on its home square) kills only that side's right. Rights never come back, even if the piece returns home.

EN PASSANT. Available only on the very next ply after an enemy pawn advances two squares from its starting rank and lands on a square horizontally adjacent to one of your pawns (your pawn must be on rank 5 as White, rank 4 as Black). Your pawn captures by moving diagonally forward onto the square the enemy pawn SKIPPED OVER, and the enemy pawn is removed from the square it actually occupies — the only capture in chess where the captured piece is not on the destination square. The right expires immediately if not exercised on that ply; it cannot be deferred. CRITICAL EDGE CASE: an en-passant capture removes two pawns from the same rank at once, which can expose your own king to an enemy rook or queen along that rank — such an en-passant is ILLEGAL and the standard make/test/unmake legality check catches it only if you actually remove the captured pawn before testing. Convention for the EP target square (matters for repetition detection): set it only when at least one pseudo-legal en-passant capture actually exists, matching modern FIDE and Lichess.

PROMOTION. A pawn reaching the far rank (8 for White, 1 for Black) MUST be replaced, as part of that same move and before the turn passes, by a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same colour. It may not remain a pawn and may not become a king. There is no limit — nine queens is legal. The promoted piece's power applies instantly, so a promotion can itself deliver check or checkmate. Underpromotion to knight is genuinely useful (forks, and stalemate-avoidance); always offer all four.

CHECK / MATE / STALEMATE. Check = the side to move's king is attacked. Checkmate = in check AND zero legal moves; that side loses immediately. Stalemate = NOT in check AND zero legal moves; the game is an immediate draw. There is no "must announce check" rule and no capturing the king.

DRAWS. (a) Stalemate. (b) Dead position / insufficient material, automatic: K vs K; K+B vs K; K+N vs K; K+B vs K+B where both bishops sit on same-coloured squares. K+N+N vs K is NOT an automatic draw (mate is possible with cooperation). (c) Fifty-move rule: maintain a halfmove clock that resets to 0 on any capture and on any pawn move; at 100 plies either player may CLAIM a draw, and at 150 plies (75 full moves) it is automatic. (d) Threefold repetition: a position repeats when the same pieces occupy the same squares, the same side is to move, the same castling rights exist, and the same en-passant possibility exists. Third occurrence = claimable; fifth = automatic. Hash positions with a Zobrist key that includes side-to-move, the four castling bits, and the EP file. (e) Mutual agreement.

NOTATION (SAN, for the move list). Piece letter K/Q/R/B/N, nothing for a pawn. Disambiguate only when two or more identical pieces could legally reach the destination: use the file letter first, the rank digit if files match, both if needed. 'x' before the destination for a capture; pawn captures write the origin file (exd5). Promotion appends '=Q'. Castling is O-O and O-O-O. Append '+' for check, '#' for mate. En passant may append 'e.p.' but usually does not.

## One-phone adaptation

PHYSICAL MODEL. One portrait phone lies FLAT on the table between the players. Only two seating orientations are supported: 0 degrees (near side) and 180 degrees (far side). Everyone on the White team sits on the near long edge, everyone on Black sits on the far edge. This mirrors the reference board exactly — ref_chess_1 has the coordinate glyphs engraved upside-down on the far two sides for precisely this reason, and we reproduce that: the a-h/1-8 labels along the top edge render rotated 180 degrees.

LAYOUT (numbers for a 390x844 CSS-px portrait viewport, scaled proportionally). The board is a square of side min(W-16, H*0.46) — 374px here — horizontally centred at x=8, vertically centred at y=235..609. That leaves a 227px band above and a 235px band below. TOP BAND (0..227) belongs to the far player and every pixel in it is drawn inside ctx.rotate(PI) about the band's centre. BOTTOM BAND (609..844) belongs to the near player, upright. Each band contains, reading outward from the board: a 22px captured-material strip, a 34px name + colour-chip + clock row in Bebas Neue, and one 48px primary action pill anchored at the band's OUTER edge (y=16..64 for the far band, y=780..828 for the near band). Anchoring actions at the outer edges means the two players' thumbs approach from opposite screen edges and the 374px board in the middle is a permanent no-touch buffer — the input zones physically cannot overlap. Only ONE band is interactive at a time: the inactive side's pill is dimmed to 0.35 alpha and its hit region is disabled, so a fidgeting opponent cannot misfire.

BOARD ORIENTATION. Default for 2 players face-to-face: board FIXED with White at the bottom, forever. Black plays "upside down" exactly as at a real board, which is correct and familiar, and it means the board never moves under anyone's finger. A "Pass-and-play in hand" toggle switches to auto-flip: the board rotates 180 degrees over 420ms after each move so the side to move always sees their army at the bottom (pieces counter-rotate so they stay upright). Never do both.

INPUT. Tap-to-select then tap-to-destination — no drag. At 374/8 = 46.75px per square a dragging finger completely occludes the piece and its neighbours; two-tap keeps the board visible. On select, the origin square lights amber, legal destinations get dots/rings, and a 92px "ghost" of the selected piece renders in the active player's HUD band as a confirmation of what is in hand. Tapping the origin again, or any illegal square, deselects. Optional "Confirm moves" setting inserts an arm/commit step (tap destination -> a green check pill appears in your own band -> tap to commit) for shaky hands. Long-press any square for 350ms to draw a coloured arrow/circle annotation, cleared on the next move.

ROTATED HIT TESTING. Because getBoundingClientRect is banned, size the single full-screen canvas to innerWidth/innerHeight at position fixed, inset 0, and convert pointer events with x = e.clientX * (canvas.width/window.innerWidth). For the rotated far band, invert manually: bx = bandW - (x - bandX), by = bandH - (y - bandY). Keep one hitRegions array of {x,y,w,h,rot,fn} and test in the region's own space.

PLAYER COUNTS 2-8. 2 = DUEL, classic hot-seat. 3-8 = RELAY: the roster is dealt alternately into a White team and a Black team, and within each team the members take that side's moves in strict round-robin. The active band shows "SAM — WHITE (2 of 3)" and a small queue of upcoming names, so nobody is surprised. Uneven teams (3, 5, 7 players) simply loop the short team faster; the solo player in a 2v1 gets a +25% clock as a handicap. 4/6/8 players unlock HAND & BRAIN, the mode that justifies the phone existing: the BRAIN of the side to move is handed the phone, sees a full-screen privacy panel listing the six piece types with counts, and secretly taps one; the phone then shows "PASS TO <HAND>", and the HAND — who never saw the tap — must make a legal move with a piece of that type (the move generator filters to that type; if no legal move of that type exists the Brain's choice is revealed and they must re-pick). Roles swap every turn.

HIDDEN INFORMATION AND THE PRIVACY SCREEN. Only Hand & Brain and the optional Sealed Move variant carry hidden information. The privacy cover is a full-bleed opaque plank of dark wood, drawn in the RECEIVING player's rotation, reading "PASS TO SAM" in Bebas Neue with a 96px circular press-and-hold disc beneath it. Content is revealed only while the finger is held down and snaps back to covered the instant it lifts, so a shoulder-surfer gets nothing from a dropped phone. A 3-2-1 countdown ring around the disc auto-covers after 6s. Base Relay and Duel have zero hidden information and never show this screen — a chess position is public by definition, and adding fake secrecy would be a lie about the game.

WHOSE TURN, AT A GLANCE. A 4px edge glow runs along the screen edge belonging to the active team (top edge for Black, bottom for White) and breathes at 3s period. Combined with the dimmed inactive pill, a player who looked away knows in under a second whether to lean in. Clocks are optional (off by default, presets 5+0, 10+0, 15+10); when on they are Space Mono, count down only for the active side, and a side under 20s turns its whole band's text to #C4432E.

WHAT WE DELIBERATELY DO NOT BUILD. No engine/AI opponent — this is a local-humans product. No in-game standings or leaderboard screen; the platform provides that. For odd counts wanting 1v1, offer KING OF THE HILL: winner stays, next challenger is pulled from a visible queue strip of names — a queue, not a table of standings.

## Drawing it with no assets

ZERO ASSETS. Everything is Path2D, gradients, and seeded noise. Two layers: a static offscreen board canvas rendered once per theme/resize, and a per-frame piece/overlay pass.

1) BOARD, rendered once into an offscreen canvas at 2x device pixels, then blitted with drawImage each frame.
 a. Frame: fill the outer rounded-rect (radius 10) with a linear gradient at ~20 degrees, #6F4327 -> #9A6540 -> #7A4A2B. Then 40 grain strokes: for each, moveTo(x0, y) and quadraticCurveTo with a +/-6px mid-control wobble across the full width, strokeStyle alternating rgba(48,28,14,0.05..0.12) and rgba(214,168,110,0.05), lineWidth 1-3. Clip to the frame ring so grain never crosses into the playfield.
 b. Bevel: 3px stroke rgba(214,168,110,0.35) along the playfield's top and left inner edges, 3px rgba(0,0,0,0.45) along bottom and right. One 1px #2A1A0E line right at the playfield boundary.
 c. 64 squares: seed a tiny LCG per square from h = (f*73856093 ^ r*19349663) >>> 0. Base fill = light ? #E9D9B4 : #A9744C, then shift lightness by (rnd()-0.5)*5% so the field breathes like real veneer. Save/clip to the square, then draw 7-10 near-horizontal grain beziers spanning the square, alternating rgba(0,0,0,0.045) and rgba(255,255,255,0.05), lineWidth 0.6-1.6, each with a per-square phase offset from the same seed and a shared grain direction that flips 90 degrees on alternating squares (real parquet boards alternate grain direction — visible in ref_chess_1). Restore.
 d. Seams: 1px rgba(0,0,0,0.06) on each square's top and left.
 e. Sheen: over the whole playfield, a linear gradient from top-left rgba(255,255,255,0.07) to transparent at 45%, then a bottom-right rgba(0,0,0,0.07) counterpart. This reproduces the single upper-left key light in every reference.
 f. Coordinates baked in: Inter 600 at 0.22*sq, files along rank 1 and ranks along file h, coloured with the opposite square's colour at 0.55 alpha. Draw the far-edge duplicate set rotated 180 degrees if you want the engraved-frame homage.
 Green theme is the same code with light #EBECD0 / dark #6F8C56, grain alpha halved and the frame switched to #2E3B33 — vinyl, not wood.

 Draw routine per piece: ctx.save(); ctx.translate(cx, cy + 0.02*sq); ctx.scale(sq/45*0.92, sq/45*0.92); ctx.translate(-22.5,-24). Contact shadow FIRST, faked without blur: five stacked ellipses centred (22.5, 39.5) with rx stepping 10->16, ry 3->5, each rgba(30,20,12,0.055). Then fill: WHITE = linearGradient(0,6)->(0,42) stops #FFFFFF, #F0E6D2 at 0.45, #D8C6A6 at 1.0; stroke #2A241F at lineWidth 1.5, lineJoin/lineCap round. BLACK = the SAME path data with fill gradient #4A423B -> #241E1A at 0.4 -> #0E0C0B, stroke #000000, and the stroke-only detail subpaths redrawn in rgba(236,232,224,0.85) so the engraved lines read against dark wood. Left specular: ctx.clip(path) then fillRect the bounding box with a horizontal gradient rgba(255,255,255,0.16) -> transparent at 45% — reproduces the bright left flank on every turned column in ref_chess_5.

3) OVERLAYS. Square highlights are plain fillRect at the alphas listed under uiChrome. Legal-move dots and capture rings are arc() calls. The check halo is six concentric stroked circles with hand-ramped alpha, since the blur/filter property is unavailable. Captured-material glyphs reuse the same Path2D set at scale sq/45*0.38.

4) HUD. All plaques are roundRect + linear gradients + two bevel strokes; no images. Player colour chips are a filled circle with a 2px #7A4A2B ring. The edge glow is a 4px fillRect with a gradient to transparent over 14px.

5) PERFORMANCE. Re-render the offscreen board only on resize or theme change. Per frame, blit the board, then draw at most 32 pieces plus overlays — under 1ms of path work on a mid phone. Cache the six Path2D objects and the four gradient objects; never construct a gradient inside the piece loop. Total code footprint for the whole piece library is under 4KB of string data.

## Risks

- Legal-move generation is where this game lives or dies. The killer cases are: castling through an attacked square (f1/g1 and d1/c1 must be safe, but b1 need only be empty); en passant that exposes the mover's own king along the 5th rank because TWO pawns leave the rank at once; and absolute pins. Do not hand-code pin logic — use pseudo-legal generate, make, is-king-attacked, unmake, and remove the EP-captured pawn before the test.
- Touch targets: at 374px board width each square is 46.75px, which is at the edge of the 44pt minimum, and edge squares sit near the screen bezel where palm rejection bites. Mitigate with tap-to-select (never drag), a 92px ghost preview of the selected piece in the player's own HUD band, and a 4px inward hit-target inset only on the outermost rank/file.
- Rotated-UI hit testing is the most likely source of silent bugs, made worse because getBoundingClientRect is banned. Every rotated region needs a manually inverted transform; a single missed inversion means the far player's Resign button lands where their Draw button appears. Build one hitRegions table with an explicit rot field and unit-test the inversion before building any screens.
- Procedural wood grain is expensive if drawn per frame. 64 squares x 9 beziers x 60fps will drop frames on a mid-range Android. It MUST be rendered once into an offscreen canvas and blitted; regenerate only on resize or theme change.
- Relay and Hand & Brain can stall socially — a 5-player game where one person is thinking has four bored people. Cap think time with an optional per-move timer (default off, but strongly recommended on for 4+), show the upcoming-turn queue permanently, and make Hand & Brain the default for even counts because it gives every player something to do every single turn.
- Threefold repetition silently fails if the position hash omits castling rights, side-to-move, or the en-passant file. Use a proper Zobrist key including all four castling bits and the EP file, and only set the EP file when a pseudo-legal EP capture actually exists, or claims will fire (or fail to fire) at the wrong moment.
